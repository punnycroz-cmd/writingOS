# THE NAME THAT SURVIVED
## CHAPTER 19: RADAR CONTACT (FULL NOVEL EDITION)

At 11:30 PM, the fragile sanctuary of the lantern room was shattered by a sharp, staccato series of electronic pings emanating from the secondary radar console.

*PING... PING... PING...*

Maren was at the terminal in a single stride, her fingers flying across the trackpad to switch the display mode from passive network monitoring to active coastal airspace and perimeter surveillance.

The screen illuminated in concentric rings of phosphor-green. 

Four distinct thermal signatures—heavy, high-emission diesel engines—were moving at high speed along the narrow, winding asphalt of the coastal highway, turning onto the overgrown access track that led toward the Point Lookout promontory.

"Four armored tactical carriers," Maren said, her voice dropping into an icy, surgical register. "And look above them."

She zoomed the optical radar sweep to a five-thousand-foot ceiling. 

Three small, triangular blips were hovering in tight triangular formation two miles out over the ocean, descending rapidly toward the lighthouse beacon.

"Combat drones," Elias said, standing beside her, his storm-gray eyes tracking their descent vectors. "Rotary-wing autonomous gunships equipped with FLIR thermal imaging and ventral miniguns. Halloway didn't just send investigators; she sent a full black-budget elimination squad."

"They triangulated our microwave burst," Maren said, calculating the velocities on the screen. "Estimated time to ground perimeter breach: eighteen minutes. The drones will reach our airspace in under six."

"That is our window," Elias said, his voice completely calm, stripped of all hesitation. "We don't try to run down the cliff path. We don't try to hide in the rocks. If we engage their Level-0 server right now, my squad and I can breach their core in twelve minutes. The moment the Black File is extracted, you initiate the broadcast macro. By the time their ground team blows the iron doors downstairs, the evidence will already be in the newsrooms of the New York Times, the Washington Post, and the Department of Justice."

Maren looked at him, her heart thumping against her ribs, but her eyes reflecting the same fierce, immovable resolve that burned in his.

She turned to the equipment locker, pulling out the pneumatic stun-carbine, racking the charging handle with a sharp, metallic *CLACK*. She checked the power cell: *98% capacity, twelve high-voltage cartridges.*

She walked over to the stairwell, slammed the secondary steel-reinforced security gate into place, and padlocked the heavy hardened-steel chain with a resolute click.

"I will hold the stairwell," Maren said, turning back to face him, the rifle held steady in her arms. "I will give you every single second you need, Elias."

Elias looked at her standing in the amber light—strong, beautiful, fearless, an embodiment of the justice her mother had died to protect.

"And if their firewalls are too thick?" she asked softly. "If we run out of time?"

Elias stepped toward the neuro-chair, his jaw setting into an iron line.

"Then we tear their empire down with us."
