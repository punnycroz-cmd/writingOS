# CHAPTER 3: THE CROW'S NEST

The headset closed around his head like a familiar hand.

Elias leaned back in the chair. The titanium ring tightened on its own, and the cool sensor pads found their places on either side of his temples. White text came up on the center monitor against a black background: *SOMNIA — Layer 1. Welcome back, Elias.* Just beneath it, a small status bar: *Neural anomaly detected. Short session recommended.*

He almost laughed. Anomaly was one way of putting it.

His heart was racing. He wasn't sure which scared him more, that the network might swallow him again, or that the people waiting on the other side would look at him and see a stranger.

He closed his eyes.

---

Layer 1 didn't come with a fall. It was more like walking through a door. One second he was sitting in his bare apartment, the next he was standing on a wooden step in air that smelled of pine resin and rain and, faintly, burned popcorn.

He opened his eyes, and his jaw dropped.

The Crow's Nest was a spaceship growing in the top of a tree. More precisely, it was a gigantic tree, its trunk as wide as an apartment building, into whose branches somebody had decided to jam a spaceship. The hull was rust-streaked green steel covered in lumpy welds, slung across three ancient branches, roots wrapped tight around engines that had gone cold long ago. Paper lanterns in every color hung along the plank walkways, and a red slide spiraled around the trunk from the upper deck all the way to the roots. On the bow, someone had painted a huge black crow, winking, with a scrawled sign: *NO GROWN-UPS. EXCEPT VIPER.*

The sky was the pale violet of a sunset that never finished setting, and far, far below lay an ocean of clouds.

"He's standing there," a girl's voice called down from above. "He's actually standing there."

Elias looked up. A girl was hanging upside down from the upper deck railing by her knees, her ponytail dangling like a broom. Her avatar had skin with a pearly blue sheen and pointed ears, and she wore an orange jumpsuit covered in pockets with a tool hanging from every one. She stared at him with big round eyes, and then those eyes went red.

"Lin," he guessed. He didn't remember her, but her username was floating over her head.

She let go, dropped straight down, flipped once in midair, landed in front of him as lightly as a cat, and threw her arms around him. Elias stood frozen, his hands hovering at his sides with no idea where to go.

"You idiot," Lin said into his shoulder. "You idiot, you idiot. Do you know I opened the Nest eleven nights in a row? Alone. Listening to Viper snore over the mic."

"I do not snore!" a deep voice roared from somewhere behind them. "That's my cooling fan!"

The planks shook. A huge figure came out of the ship's hold, well over six and a half feet tall, wearing scuffed gunmetal titanium armor, shoulders as broad as a doorframe. But the first thing Elias noticed was the pink floral apron tied around the armor's waist, printed with *WORLD'S BEST DAD*.

Viper stopped three steps away. The face under the helmet was square, with a thick beard and a scar running through one eyebrow, and it was twitching like it hadn't decided whether to be furious or thrilled.

"Eleven days," Viper said.

"I know."

"Not one message."

"I was in a coma."

Viper was silent for a moment. Then he took the last three steps, reached out with both armored arms, wrapped up Elias and Lin together, and lifted them both off the deck.

"You stupid monkey," Viper muttered. "You scared me half to death."

"Viper," Lin groaned. "Ribs. My ribs. Virtual ribs still hurt."

Viper set them down. Elias staggered, and when he got his balance back, he saw the third one.

Ghost_Kev stood leaning against the trunk a little distance away. His avatar was slight, in a gray hooded cloak, a long sniper rifle slung across his back. His face gave almost nothing away. Only his eyes moved, sweeping Elias from head to foot, slow and careful, as if reading a report.

"You don't remember us," Kev said. It wasn't a question.

The whole deck went quiet. Lin spun around to look at Kev, then at Elias. Viper's grin froze in place.

Elias could have lied. He could have pretended. But he looked at the three of them, at the pink apron, at Lin's red eyes, at the way Kev stood a little apart like he'd already braced himself for bad news, and he couldn't do it.

"No," he said. "I don't."

Wings beat overhead. A small crow, its black feathers glinting blue, its eyes ringed with a dull silver glow, swooped down from a branch and landed on the railing right beside him. It tilted its head and looked at him, one side and then the other, and gave a short, hoarse caw, like a question. Elias looked at it. It looked at him. Nothing passed between them. The crow hopped back one step, cawed again, then flapped off, circled the hull once, and disappeared into the leaves.

"That was Coal," Lin said softly, her voice shaking. "Your Echo. He rode on your shoulder for three years. The Crow's Nest is named after him." She bit her lip. "Echoes know their owners by brainwave signature. I guess... yours changed."

Elias looked where the crow had gone. He didn't remember it, but his chest gave a sharp little pang, like missing a stair in the dark.

At Viper's feet, a glowing orange bulldog wearing a pink bow that matched the apron snuffled at Elias's shoes, then plopped down and stuck its tongue out. At least one of them didn't think he was a stranger.

"Brisket," Viper said. "Don't feed him. He can't eat, but he begs anyway."

---

They sat around a table made from an old ship's wing on the middle deck, under a swaying cluster of lanterns. Viper carried out a steaming pot of something he insisted on calling Texas chili, and even though nobody in Layer 1 could actually eat, the smell still made Elias's stomach growl.

Before Elias could say anything, Kev raised a hand. On his shoulder perched a gray paper crane, Kev's Echo. It folded its wings and the lights in its eyes went out. "Bubbles. Brisket. Sleep," Kev said. The bulldog yawned and flopped flat on the deck. A snow-white cat that had been curled in Lin's lap the whole time, blowing soap bubbles out of its ears, drowsily closed its eyes too.

"Echoes hear everything," Kev explained to Elias. "They're useful for a lot. Translating, looking things up, reminders, keeping Viper's kid Rosie company. But they're Halloway's. Lin modified them so they don't send anything back to the server while we're in the Nest. I still don't really trust it." He shrugged. "Old habit."

Then Elias told them. Not everything, because he didn't know everything himself. The hospital, the eleven days, the thirty years shattering in his head, the chart that said *Subject Zero*, the contract under the bed, the black sedan.

Lin hugged her knees and listened without blinking. Viper rested two huge fists on the table, and when Elias got to the part about *permanent removal of the nightmares*, he slammed one down so hard the chili pot jumped.

"Bastards," Viper said through his teeth. "They knew. They knew damn well you'd trade anything to get rid of that thing."

"You knew about the nightmare?" Elias asked.

"You told us once," Kev said. "Two years ago, after the regional final, when you were drunk. You said when you were a kid you knocked over a candle and burned down your foster family's house, and your foster mother died in the fire. You said you dreamed about it every night." He paused. "Then you never brought it up again, and we never asked."

Elias nodded slowly. The notebook, the photo, the Ashford house. It all fit.

"So what was I like? Before?"

The three of them looked at each other.

"Honestly? You were super hard to like," Lin said.

"Lin."

"I'm being honest! For the first three months he said nothing to me except *left*, *right*, and *out of ammo*. I thought he was a bot." She sniffed. "Then one time I got surrounded by a whole team in the semifinal, and he left his position and ran across half the map to pull me out. We lost the match. He just said, *Lose a match, you play it again. Lose a person, you don't.*"

Viper nodded. "You were the best I ever saw in Vanguard. Read a map like a book. Except you never once came outside. Three years, and we've never seen your real face."

"We've never seen each other's real faces," Kev said.

"Sure," Viper said, "but I still know you snack at three in the morning, Kev, because I hear the crinkling over the mic."

"That's my mouse."

"Mice don't eat cheese puffs."

Lin burst out laughing, a clear, sudden peal, and Elias realized he was laughing too. The joke wasn't that funny. It was just that something warm was rising in his chest, something he didn't have a name for. These three bickered like siblings, and they had kept a seat at this table empty for him for eleven days.

He didn't remember them, but he thought maybe he could learn them again.

---

"There's something," he said, once the chili had gone cold. "I need to find someone."

He told them about the name and the two faces. Lin had a translucent screen open in the air before he finished, her fingers moving so fast they blurred.

"Maren," she muttered. "Last name? Age? Job?"

"Don't know. Close to thirty. Dark hair, dark eyes."

"Oh, sure, there's only about a million of those." She kept typing. "Okay, Marens within three hundred miles, three hundred and twelve results. Filter by age, a hundred and forty." She wrinkled her nose. "Can you draw her? Somnia can render from memory."

Elias closed his eyes and called up the woman's face: the window, the sunlight, the mug, the smile. He reached into the air, and the features came up under his fingertips, blurry and then clear, like a photo developing in a tray. When he opened his eyes, the face was floating over the table, life-sized, its dark eyes looking at someone outside the frame.

"She's beautiful," Viper said quietly.

Lin ran a match. The progress bar crept forward, ten percent, fifty, ninety, and stopped.

*No matching results found.*

"That's ridiculous," Lin said. She ran it again, widened the radius, then dropped the name filter entirely. Still nothing. "There's no Maren with this face anywhere. Not in any public database I can get into, anyway."

Elias looked at the floating face, and something heavy slid down into his stomach. Maybe she wasn't real. Maybe she was just a piece of those thirty fake years, someone his brain had built and then left behind when everything shattered. Maybe the thing he'd been clutching like a pebble was only a grain of sand.

Then he saw Kev get up.

Kev walked over to the face, slowly, as if afraid of breaking it, tilted his head, and narrowed his eyes.

"Kev?" Lin said.

"I've seen this face," Kev said.

Elias was on his feet. "Where?"

Kev didn't answer right away. He opened his own screen and dug through an archive, his mouth pressed tight. The three of them waited while the sunset wind moved through the branches and tipped the lanterns sideways.

"I read a lot of news. Old habit," Kev said, his eyes still on the screen. "In February there was a small item in a local paper. An investigative journalist lost at sea. His boat broke up on the reef off the north shore. They never found a body." He pulled a photo into the middle of the table and set it next to the face Elias had drawn. "This ran with the story about the memorial."

The photo showed a group of people in black standing on a wooden pier, their hair whipped by wind. In the middle, a little apart from the others, stood a woman looking out at the sea, her face in profile, not smiling. But that chin, those eyes, there was no mistaking them.

Nobody spoke. Elias looked at the two faces side by side, one smiling by a window, one standing still on a pier, and something in his head clicked into place, like a latch dropping into its catch.

"What was the journalist's name?" he asked.

"Corwin Daly, thirty-one. The last story he published was about military contracts with..." Kev paused and glanced at Elias. "...Halloway Neuro-Systems."

Viper swore under his breath.

"And her? Does it say who she is?"

Kev enlarged the caption under the photo.

> *Corwin Daly's fiancée, memory forensics specialist Lena Hale, at the memorial at Bayside Harbor.*

"Lena," Lin read aloud. "Not Maren."

Elias looked at the name. Lena Hale. It stirred nothing, no pebble, no click, just two unfamiliar words under a photograph. But the face couldn't be wrong. He was as sure of it as he was of breathing.

"I need to see her," he said.

"Whoa, whoa, hold on," Viper said, raising a hand the size of a shovel. "You got out of the hospital this morning. There's a black car outside your building. Your chart says in plain English they let you out to see where you'd go. And now you want to walk straight up to the fiancée of a missing reporter who was investigating that exact company?"

Elias said nothing. Viper was right, every word of it.

"There's something else," Kev said, his voice dropping. "I should have said it first. While you were gone, someone was asking about you on an anonymous forum. Posted a bounty for anyone who knew where you were."

"Halloway?"

"No. Halloway's people don't need a bounty. They knew exactly where you were all along. This person didn't. The account had no history, left no trace, and signed with one word."

"What word?"

"*Nyx.*"

The wind moved through the Crow's Nest again. The lanterns swung and then settled. Far below, the ocean of clouds rolled slowly, as if it were breathing.

Elias looked at the woman on the pier one more time.

"Get me the address where she works," he said.
