# THE NAME THAT SURVIVED
## CHAPTER 12: THE PURSUIT (FULL NOVEL EDITION)

At 11:45 AM, the violent screech of heavy rubber tires skidding across wet asphalt shattered the fragile peace of the motel room.

Maren was on her feet before the sound had even faded. She crossed the room in two silent strides, pressing her back against the damp plaster wall beside the window and peeling back a single plastic slat of the blinds by a fraction of an inch.

Her breath seized in her throat.

Two matte-black commercial SUVs had jumped the curb, parking diagonally across the driveway to form an impenetrable barricade. Six men in dark tactical windbreakers, ballistic vests, and low-profile tactical headsets were fanning out across the courtyard. Two carried suppressed semi-automatic sidearms held at the low-ready; the other four swept the exterior doors with directional microwave radio-scanners, their handheld antennas homing in on the electromagnetic frequency of Maren’s laptop.

"They tracked the Layer-2 handshake," Maren hissed, dropping the blind. Her voice was pure ice, but her pupils were dilated with adrenaline. "We have less than thirty seconds before they breach the door."

"Pack the field kit," Elias said. 

His physical weakness vanished in an instant, overridden by the cold, razor-sharp survival instinct of a competitive combat point-man. He grabbed the carbon-fiber medical case and Maren’s laptop satchel in one motion, slinging the heavy straps across his chest.

He moved into the tiny, windowless bathroom. 

Above the rusted metal shower stall was a narrow, frosted-glass transom window designed for ventilation, leading out onto the steep, overgrown earthen embankment behind the motel.

Elias stepped onto the edge of the ceramic basin, raised his right combat boot, and slammed his heel into the center of the frosted glass.

*CRACK.*

The safety glass shattered into a thousand blunt, granular fragments, tumbling out into the wet ferns behind the building. Elias used his gloved forearm to sweep the jagged edges clear of the aluminum frame.

"Up," he ordered, turning and locking his hands together to form a stirrup. "I'll hoist you through. Hit the dirt and stay low."

Maren didn't hesitate. She stepped her boot into his interlaced fingers. With a single, explosive heave, Elias lifted her upward. Maren caught the aluminum sill with both forearms, wiggled her slender frame through the narrow opening, and slid down the muddy slope into the dense undergrowth.

Inside the motel room, the front door was blasted off its hinges with a deafening *BOOM* as a breaching shotgun obliterated the deadbolt.

"CLEAR LEFT!" a voice roared through the dust. "BATHROOM! TARGET FLEEING NORTH!"

Elias grabbed the sill, pulled his heavy frame up through the transom with raw, adrenaline-fueled strength, and tumbled headfirst out the window just as a burst of suppressed 9mm rounds chewed through the bathroom door, shredding the drywall into a blizzard of white powder.

He hit the muddy embankment, rolled onto his feet, and grabbed Maren by the hand.

"Down the ravine!" he shouted over the roar of the rain.

They scrambled through the dense, rain-soaked briars, sliding and tumbling down a forty-foot mud embankment into the dense pine forest that flanked the secondary access road. Thorny vines ripped at the sleeves of Elias's navy coat; branches whipped across Maren’s face, leaving thin, stinging scratches across her cheekbones.

Behind them, at the crest of the ridge, three tactical operators appeared through the trees, their suppressed carbines snapping in sharp, rhythmic coughing bursts.

*Pfft-pfft-pfft.*

High-velocity rounds cracked through the wet pine canopy above their heads, shredding needles and severing branches that rained down like green shrapnel.

"Fifty yards to the treeline!" Maren shouted, her breath coming in ragged gasps.

She pulled her key fob from her pocket, pressing the remote unlock. 

Fifty yards ahead, hidden beneath a dense canopy of low-hanging weeping willows, the amber hazard lights of her dark-gray sedan flashed twice in the gloom.

They broke from the treeline at a full sprint.

Just as they reached the car, the lead SUV rounded the gravel bend sixty yards up the access road, its high-beam halogen lights piercing the fog like twin laser beams.

"Get in!" Elias yelled.

They tumbled into the vehicle. Maren slammed the passenger door shut, throwing her satchel into the footwell. 

Elias hit the ignition button. The six-cylinder engine roared to life with a ferocious, throaty growl. He slammed the gear selector into drive, floored the accelerator, and dumped the clutch.

*SCREEECH.*

The tires spun wildly on the wet gravel, spraying mud and stone across the windshield of the approaching SUV, before the rubber finally bit into the asphalt of the main coastal highway.

The sedan surged forward like a missile, its rear end fishtailing violently across both lanes before Elias corrected the steering with the effortless, millimeter precision of a reflex-trained racer.

Behind them, the two black SUVs accelerated in furious pursuit, their pursuit lights flashing red and blue through the blinding gray fog.

Elias glanced in the rearview mirror, his storm-gray eyes narrowing into slits of pure, cold focus.

"Hold on, Maren," he said softly, shifting into fifth gear as the needle climbed past ninety miles per hour. "We're taking the cliffs."
