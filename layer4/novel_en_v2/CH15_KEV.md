# CHAPTER 15: KEV

Kevin Tran slept an average of four hours a night, and he had slept that way since he was twenty-one.

The doctors at the VA called it circadian disruption following time-dilation exposure. Kev called it "the broken clock." His body had once lived two full years inside seven days, and ever since then it hadn't trusted any clock at all. Some nights he lay down at eleven, closed his eyes, opened them, and it was light out, and he wasn't sure whether he'd slept eight hours or eight years. So he didn't sleep much. He worked.

At three in the morning, Viper's study smelled of electronics dust and coffee brewed the night before. Kev sat in front of three monitors with his feet pulled up on the chair and a half-eaten bag of cheese puffs beside the keyboard. He hadn't meant to bring it. It had just been sitting in his bag when he left home.

The gray paper crane perched on top of the middle monitor, wings folded, eyes dark. Since they'd come to Ashgrove he had kept it in sleep mode, and Viper's and Linh's Echoes too. Two years ago Linh had modified their cores so they wouldn't send anything back to the server while they were in the Crow's Nest, and of everyone Kev knew, Linh was the best at that kind of thing. But Kev had worked in a building where they used Echoes to watch their own soldiers. He didn't believe the things were asleep. He believed they were under orders not to talk.

The left monitor had three years of Vanguard records open. The middle one had Elias's contract, photographed page by page. The right one was running a program Kev had written himself, scanning all the network traffic around this house for the last forty-eight hours, looking for anything that didn't belong.

He was looking for whoever had recruited Elias.

---

Nobody gets invited into Layer 4 out of nowhere. Kev understood that better than anyone.

In 2013 he was nineteen, a third-string shooter in an amateur neural gaming league in Ohio. A man in a wool overcoat came to watch the regional final, bought him a can of soda, and asked if he'd like to play in a bigger league. Vanguard. A salary, insurance, a future.

Two years later he signed with an Army "capability development" program running on Layer 3, a hundred times faster than real time. Seven days outside, two years inside. Two years of combat training, marches, hunger, cold, watching his squadmates die and respawn and die again, in a box made of light that they called a "safe simulated environment."

When he woke up, his mother was standing by the bed crying, because he hadn't recognized her right away. It was only three seconds, three seconds of looking at her like a stranger, and she still brings it up every Tết.

After that he was cut from the program. The paperwork said "failed to meet psychological stability index." He didn't argue. Maybe they were right.

Vanguard wasn't a league. It was a net. The military sponsored the league, companies like Halloway provided the platform, and every season a few dozen of the best players got "invited to a meeting." Kev knew because he had been one of them. Five years later he recognized the man in the wool overcoat in a photo from a defense conference, standing right next to a general.

So when Elias told them about the contract under his bed, Kev didn't ask *why did Elias agree*. He asked *who got to Elias first*.

---

It took him three days to find the answer, and it was somewhere he hadn't expected.

There was no man in a wool coat. There was no can of soda. Elias had never met anyone. Kev had reread the team's entire chat history back to 2021, and not once had Elias mentioned a recruiter.

Instead there were small things, each of them nothing on its own.

March 2022: Elias's Echo, the crow named Coal, started recommending a series of videos about memory restructuring therapy. Kev found traces of it in the chat log, where Elias grumbled to Linh that "the crow keeps putting on these doctor videos."

September 2022: Elias switched therapists. The new one was named Pruitt, part of Halloway's network of affiliated clinics, "suggested by your Echo based on your sleep patterns."

January 2023: Pruitt increased his dose of sleeping pills, and the nightmares didn't get better. They got worse.

June 2023: the team was suddenly sponsored for the new season by a fund called Lumen Neural Ventures. Kev traced Lumen's parent company to Halloway Neuro-Systems.

February 2024: Elias told Viper that "a doctor" had mentioned a new trial that could get rid of the nightmares for good. Viper told him not to trust it. Elias didn't say anything else.

August 2024: the contract.

Kev looked at the timeline he'd laid out on the screen. Three years. No force, no threats. Just a crow sitting on his shoulder, suggesting one video at a time, introducing one doctor at a time, listening to every three a.m. complaint, and sending all of it back to someone waiting at the end of the road. They hadn't needed to lure Elias. They had only needed to wait.

Kev picked up the bag of cheese puffs, looked at it for a while, and put it down without eating any.

---

At 4:10 the network scanner on the right monitor beeped.

Kev flinched and nearly knocked over his coffee. A red line was blinking. He pulled it up and enlarged it.

For the last forty-eight hours, this house had been emitting exactly what it should: Viper's router, Rosa's phone next door, the smart TV in the living room, Rosie's pink tablet. Nothing unusual, except one signal.

It was extremely weak, on a low frequency band no civilian device used. It didn't transmit continuously. It flared once every forty minutes for about a tenth of a second, like a heartbeat with long gaps between. And it wasn't coming from any device in the house.

Kev traced the source. It took twenty minutes, because the signal was so weak and Viper's house was all old wooden walls. He made a circuit of the house in the dark with his laptop in his hands and a homemade antenna plugged into the USB port, creeping along so he wouldn't wake anyone, like a burglar sneaking through his own friend's house.

Living room, no. Rosie's room, no. Viper's room, no. Lena's room, no.

In the study, where Elias was asleep on the cot, the signal jumped the moment Kev stepped through the doorway.

Elias was lying on his side facing the wall, one arm hanging off the edge of the cot, asleep like the dead, the sleep of someone completely spent.

Kev stood in the doorway with the laptop, looking at the red dot on the screen. It wasn't in a corner, or at an outlet, or in the computer rig. It was right in the middle of the bed.

He took two more steps. The dot darkened. He lowered the laptop to the level of Elias's head. It darkened further.

The signal flared once more, a tenth of a second, and this time Kev could read the identifier attached to it, one he'd seen just yesterday on the photo of Elias's chart.

*ZERO-ALPHA-7.*

The Alpha-7 Monitoring Protocol. *Neural data to be transmitted directly to Dr. I. Halloway.*

There was no device. There was no chip under the skin. Kev had checked the surgical records, and Lena had scanned him already. The signal was coming from Elias's brain itself. Maybe they'd installed it when he was a child, trained his brain to put out a wave pattern that could be picked up from a distance, the way you band a bird's leg. Maybe it was an aftereffect of Layer 4. Kev didn't know. He only knew it was transmitting, like clockwork, every forty minutes, right now.

The black sedan hadn't followed Elias to the laundromat. Nobody had tailed the taco truck. They hadn't needed to. They only had to wait for the next signal.

Kev sat down on the floor with his back against the doorframe and the laptop on his knees. He thought about Rosie asleep across the hall, hugging her stuffed dinosaur. He thought about Viper driving two hours in the middle of the night. He thought about the three seconds his mother brought up every Tết.

---

At six in the morning Elias woke up and saw Kev sitting on the floor by the door. He didn't startle. He just lay still and looked at Kev, as if he'd already guessed something was wrong.

"Say it," he said.

Kev told him everything: the crow and the videos, Dr. Pruitt, Lumen Neural Ventures, the signal that went out every forty minutes. He told it briefly and evenly, adding nothing, the way he always told bad news. He'd learned that inside the box of light, all the times he'd had to tell someone that the person standing next to them had just died for the seventeenth time.

Elias sat up, put his feet on the floor, propped his elbows on his knees, and listened to all of it without interrupting.

"Since when?" he asked when Kev was done.

"I don't know. Maybe since you were a kid. Maybe only since Layer 4."

"They know I'm here."

"Almost certainly."

"They know she's here."

Kev didn't answer. He didn't need to.

Elias put his head in his hands, fingers pushed into his hair, and stayed like that for a long time. Outside the window it was getting light. There were birds, and the paper boy's bicycle rattling past the end of the street.

"Why haven't they come?" he asked, his voice muffled behind his hands.

Kev had been thinking about that question all night. "Because they don't need to," he said. "Not yet. You're still doing exactly what they want."

"What?"

"Pulling her close to you." Kev looked down at the screen, where the red dot was waiting for the next pulse. "I reread the 2004 report from Lena's decrypt. *The Zero–One pair is the only pair to achieve stable two-way synchronization.* They need two people, not one. Every day she dives with you, your two brains lock together a little more. They're letting you do the hardest part for them."

Elias looked up. His eyes were red but completely dry.

"I have to leave," he said. "Get away from here. Away from her. Away from Rosie."

"They'll follow you. And they've already seen her. Halloway went to her office in person." Kev shook his head. "If you leave, only one thing happens. She stays here alone, with nobody watching her back."

"Then what do I do?"

"I'm working on a way to mask the signal. It takes time. I need to know where it's being generated." Kev paused. "And I need Lena. She understands your brain better than anyone."

Elias stared at the floor, at a long scratch in the floorboards, probably where Rosie had dragged some toy. He looked at it like he was counting every grain in the wood.

"Don't tell her," he said.

Kev waited a moment. "She has a right to know."

"I know." Elias still didn't look up. "Just give me a few days. Until you find a way to mask it. If she finds out now, she'll..." He trailed off.

Kev knew what he was going to say. *She'll look at me differently. She'll realize that the one person left in the world who remembers her is also the one who led them right to her door.* Kev had seen that look on someone's face before. Only three seconds, but enough to never forget it.

"A few days," Kev said. "If I haven't found a way by then, you tell her yourself. Or I will."

Elias nodded.

Kev got up and closed his laptop. At the door, he stopped.

"You know who you remind me of?" he asked.

Elias looked at him.

"Me," Kev said. "When I first got out of that box. I carried everything myself, didn't let anybody know. Took me three years to figure out it didn't protect anyone."

He didn't wait for Elias to answer. He went straight down to the kitchen and started a fresh pot of coffee, and while he poured the water into the machine, he noticed his hands were shaking a little.

On the refrigerator door, between one of Rosie's dinosaur drawings and the school calendar, there was a note held up by a pineapple magnet, in Viper's handwriting: *KEV — EAT BREAKFAST. NOT CHEESE PUFFS.*

Kev looked at the note for a long moment, then opened the fridge and took out two eggs.
