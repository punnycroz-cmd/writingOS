# THE NAME THAT SURVIVED
## CHAPTER 4: THE FIRST SESSION (FULL NOVEL EDITION)

The examination room was separated from the main office by an eight-millimeter pane of sound-damped laminated glass. Inside, the ambient illumination was adjusted to a low, warm amber—specifically calibrated to two thousand Kelvin to suppress visual cortex hyperactivity.

Elias sat in the reclining leather neuro-chair. His soaked navy coat hung from a steel rack in the corner, dripping a steady, rhythmic cadence into a plastic tray. Beneath the coat, his broad shoulders were hunched slightly, his elbows resting on his knees, his scarred hands clasped tightly between them.

Maren stood beside the equipment cart, her movements brisk, methodical, and shielded behind the armor of clinical routine. 

She peeled open a sterile alcohol wipe, its sharp, antiseptic tang biting into the air. 

"Lean your head back against the headrest, Mr. Vane," she instructed, her voice steady despite the rapid rhythm of her pulse beneath her turtleneck. "Keep your chin level. I need to prepare the dermal contact points."

Elias leaned back. The dark leather groaned beneath his weight.

Maren stepped closer. Her fingers parted the damp, dark strands of hair at his left temple. When the alcohol swab touched his skin, a sharp, microscopic tremor rippled across his jawline. His eyelids fluttered, but he did not pull away.

Maren felt the heat radiating from his skin. It was not a normal biological fever; it was the distinct, localized thermal signature of severe metabolic exhaustion in the cerebral cortex. His temples were burning to the touch, while his hands were as cold as river stones.

"I need to state the legal protocol for the audio record," Maren said, her voice adopting the rhythmic, formal cadence of a forensic examiner. "This is an uncertified, exploratory neural scan conducted outside standard clinical operating hours. I am not prescribing medication. I am performing a baseline verification to determine if foreign, synthetic, or corrupted memory artifacts are present in your neural pathways."

"Understood," Elias whispered.

"If I detect evidence of active neuro-chemical poisoning, criminal memory erasure, or unlawful behavioral conditioning, I am mandated by federal statute to preserve the raw telemetry and file an immediate report with the Department of Health."

Elias opened his storm-gray eyes. He looked straight up into her face—so close that she could see the flecks of dark charcoal ringing his pupils.

"If you find anything in my head that belongs to someone else..." he said, his voice raw, stripped of all defenses, "...I want you to tell me the truth. Even if it destroys whatever is left of me."

Maren held his gaze for three long seconds. In her five years of forensic practice, she had examined corporate spies, traumatized soldiers, and desperate criminals attempting to fabricate alibis. She had seen fear, arrogance, deception, and greed. 

She had never seen a man look at his own mind with such terrifying, unvarnished humility.

"I deal exclusively in the truth, Mr. Vane," she said quietly. "It's the only currency I accept."

She reached for the primary sensor band—a curved arc of matte-gray titanium embedded with twelve micro-quantum transceivers and six cryogenic dermal nodes. She settled the band across his brow, adjusting the tension knob until the six nodes clicked softly into place against his temporal and frontal bones.

"Close your eyes," Maren commanded gently. "Breathe slowly in through your nose for four counts, hold for two, exhale through your mouth for six. Count backwards from one hundred."

Elias closed his eyes. His breathing began to slow, matching the rhythmic cadence of the room.

Maren stepped back to the master workstation and tapped the primary activation sequence.

The console hummed. The twin curved monitors flashed, cascading a waterfall of raw telemetry down the screen in glowing lines of cyan and amber text:

> **`[NEURAL TELEMETRY LINK: STABLE]`**  
> **`[SAMPLE RATE: 10,000 HZ / QUANTUM COHERENCE: 94.2%]`**  
> **`[DELTA RHYTHM: 2.4 HZ — SUB-SURFACE]`**  
> **`[THETA-GAMMA COUPLING: ABNORMAL HIGH DENSITY — 6.8 HZ TO 42 HZ]`**

Behind the glass partition, Nia leaned over the secondary display, her coffee mug suspended mid-air. "Look at the synaptic density in the prefrontal cortex," Nia whispered through the intercom headset. "Maren... that’s not the brain of an amnesia patient. Look at the theta-gamma phase-locking. It’s like looking at a supercomputer operating on idle. His baseline neural plasticity is off the charts."

"He said he was in Layer 4," Maren murmured, her fingers dancing across the touch-sensitive sliders, tuning the holographic decoder. "A brain has to possess an extraordinary baseline plasticity threshold to even survive the entry sequence."

"Do you believe him?" Nia asked. "Layer 4 is supposed to be a myth. Corporate urban legend."

"The telemetry doesn't lie, Nia," Maren said. "Engaging visual decoder. Let's see what survived the crash."

Maren swiped her hand across the central trackpad.

Above the desk, the three-dimensional holographic projector hummed to life, projecting a four-foot sphere of shimmering, pale-blue light into the center of the room.

At first, there was only static—chaotic, pulsing fractals of white noise generated by damaged synaptic pathways. But as Maren fine-tuned the temporal filter, the noise resolved into fluid, impressionistic fragments of high-resolution imagery:

*A child's small hand reaching toward an old wooden window frame covered in thick winter frost.*  
*The sound of heavy, torrential rain drumming against a rusted corrugated tin roof.*  
*A dimly lit corridor smelling of floor wax and boiled cabbage.*

"These are childhood episodic traces," Nia observed over the headset. "Early developmental encoding. Ages five to seven."

And then, the audio decoder caught a frequency.

It was not a spoken word. It was a melody.

A woman's voice, low, gravelly, and unhurried, humming a slow, haunting lullaby behind a heavy wooden door. The notes were minor, melancholic, rising in a subtle, three-step cadence that hovered between Sephardic folk and classical modal scales.

Maren’s hands froze over the keyboard.

The blood drained from her face so fast that her vision tunneled into blackness. Her heart stopped, seized in the grip of an icy, crushing fist.

"Maren?" Nia’s voice crackled through the intercom, suddenly alarmed. "Maren, what's wrong? Your biometric tracker just redlined!"

Maren couldn't speak. She couldn't breathe.

That melody.

It was *La Nana de la Nieve*—an obscure Sephardic lullaby that her mother, Elena Solis, had sung to her every single night in the nursery before the fire twenty years ago. It was a song passed down through four generations of their family, never recorded on digital media, never written down in sheet music, never sung outside the four walls of the house that had burned to ash when Maren was nine years old.

"Where did you get that?" Maren whispered, her voice cracking as she lunged toward the reclining chair. 

She grabbed the leather headrest, leaning over Elias until her face was barely inches from his. 

"Elias! Open your eyes!" she demanded, her voice shaking with a mixture of terror and furious desperation. "Who taught you that song?! Whose voice is that in your head?!"

In the chair, Elias’s eyelids fluttered frantically. Beneath the titanium band, his brow furrowed in agony. His hands gripped the leather armrests with such violent force that the stitching began to rip.

"I... I don't know..." he groaned, his voice trapped in the grip of a waking nightmare. "It's... it's the fire... she's at the door..."

On the holographic projector, the imagery shattered. The peaceful childhood window dissolved into a swirling, blinding vortex of thick, black smoke and roaring orange flame:

*A concrete hallway illuminated by the strobing red flash of industrial emergency beacons.*  
*A heavy steel fire door with a massive brass locking bar.*  
*A woman with severe burn scars stretching across her left forearm, her face smudged with soot, her dark eyes blazing with desperate, maternal fury as she shoved a trembling, seven-year-old boy through an air duct.*  
*The woman's voice tearing through the deafening roar of the inferno:*  
***“Tên em là Maren! Nhớ lấy, đừng bao giờ quên em! CHẠY ĐI!”***

"MOM!" Maren screamed, an involuntary, childish cry tearing from her throat.

Before she could reach for the controls, a catastrophic neuro-electrical surge detonated through the titanium sensor band.

*CRACK.*

A bright blue arc of electrical static discharged from the dermal nodes. The master console let out a piercing, continuous alarm shriek as the holographic sphere imploded into a shower of sparkling red telemetry errors.

Elias gasped, his back arching off the chair as his storm-gray eyes snapped open, wide with sheer terror.

A thick, dark stream of blood poured from his left nostril, dripping down his lip and splashing onto the collar of his hospital shirt.

Maren stood frozen beside the chair, her hands trembling violently, her chest heaving, staring down at the blood on his face as if looking at a ghost that had crawled out of her own burning grave.
