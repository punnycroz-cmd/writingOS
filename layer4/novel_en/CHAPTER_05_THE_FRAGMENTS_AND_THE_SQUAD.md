# THE NAME THAT SURVIVED
## CHAPTER 5: THE FRAGMENTS & THE SQUAD (FULL NOVEL EDITION)

Midnight arrived with the sound of steel cables howling in the gale outside.

Across the industrial canal, the rain beat a frantic, uneven rhythm against the reinforced wire-glass panes of Elias’s top-floor studio apartment. It was a barren, spartan space—concrete floors polished to a dull gray, a single unmade cot in the corner, and a kitchen counter that held nothing except an electric kettle, a jar of instant coffee, and three empty blister packs of over-the-counter analgesics.

The only real living presence in the room was the ambient blue glow emanating from his custom-built, liquid-cooled gaming workstation. Three curved thirty-two-inch monitors sat angled on a heavy industrial workbench, their coolant lines glowing with faint ultraviolet luminescence.

Elias sat in his ergonomic chair, a wad of blood-soaked paper towels pressed against his left nostril. 

Every pulse of his heart sent a rhythmic spike of searing, white-hot agony through his optic chiasm. When he blinked, his peripheral vision was fringed with shimmering halos of digital static—the unmistakable signature of synaptic hyper-excitation. 

On the center display, the private, peer-to-peer encrypted voice channel of his Neuro-Gaming Squad was active.

"You're bleeding again, aren't you?" Cipher_Lin’s synthesized voice crackled through Elias’s over-ear headset. "I can hear the nasal occlusion in your voice, Vane. Don't lie to me."

Elias tossed the crimson-stained paper into a metal wastebasket beside the desk. "Just a localized vascular burst. The scanner’s feedback loop tripped my temporal threshold."

"Localized vascular burst my ass!" Viper_99’s voice boomed over the channel, accompanied by the mechanical clack of a virtual weapon charging in the background. His avatar—a hulking, titanium-clad juggernaut—was idling in a tactical staging lobby on the left screen. "You pulled a four-hundred-gigabyte telemetry dump through an unshielded forensic rig tonight! If you keep redlining your cerebral cortex like this, Elias, your temporal lobe is going to look like scrambled eggs before the week is out!"

"She recognized the lullaby," Elias said quietly, his voice cutting through their banter like a cold blade.

The voice channel went dead silent.

On the right monitor, the tactical avatar of Ghost_Kev—the squad’s sniper and network security architect—stopped pacing.

"The doctor?" Kev asked, his voice dropping into serious, analytical focus. "Dr. Maren Solis? The woman whose name you woke up with?"

"It wasn't just a name," Elias murmured, resting his elbows on the desk, his trembling fingers pressing into his temples. "When she hooked me up to the Layer-2 decoder, my subconscious rendered an audio file. An obscure folk melody. The moment she heard it, she almost fainted. It was a song her mother used to sing to her before she died."

"Jesus," Viper muttered. "How the hell does an unrecorded childhood song from another person’s dead mother end up inside your hippocampus?"

"Because of the fire," Elias said, staring at the blue cooling tubes inside his computer tower. "When the scanner pushed deeper, I saw a memory. A real one. Billowing black smoke. An industrial hallway. And a woman with severe burn scars on her arm pushing a seven-year-old boy through a ventilation duct. She screamed Maren’s name. She told the boy to run. And when I tried to look at her face... the machine hit a cryptographic wall. A feedback shock that almost blew out my optical nerve."

"That’s not a natural memory block," Cipher_Lin said, the rapid-fire clatter of her mechanical keyboard resuming with furious speed. "That's an active corporate firewall. Someone didn't just wipe your memories, Elias—they installed an active countermeasure in that specific sector. If anyone tries to read the identity of that woman, the protocol triggers a neuro-vascular seizure to fry the host's brain."

"Military-grade black-box conditioning," Kev agreed grimly. "Listen to me, Elias. While you were offline, Lin and I ran an automated scraper through the county archives and the corporate registry. We cross-referenced Elena Solis."

"What did you find?" Elias asked, his heart thudding against his ribs.

"Elena Solis was a senior biochemist at a private research laboratory called the Prometheus Bio-Cognitive Institute," Lin explained, pulling up a series of archived public records on Elias’s center screen. "Twenty years ago, on the night of October fourteenth, that facility burned to the ground. The official fire department report listed it as an accidental chemical explosion caused by a gas line rupture. One casualty: Elena Solis."

"And who owned the Prometheus Institute?" Elias asked, though he already knew the answer.

"An early-stage shell corporation registered in Delaware," Lin said. "The majority shareholder and lead project director was a thirty-four-year-old neuro-biologist named **Dr. Iris Halloway**."

Elias leaned back in his chair, the breath whistling through his teeth.

The architecture of the conspiracy was terrifyingly complete. 

Twenty years ago, Halloway was running an illegal experiment on orphan children. Elena Solis tried to stop it, tried to save them, and was murdered in the fire. Halloway recovered the surviving child—Elias—and used an early prototype of the dream technology to rewrite his brain, planting a false memory of guilt to keep him docile and broken. And for twenty years, she had watched him, groomed him, waiting until his unique neural pathways matured so she could use him to unlock Layer 4.

*BZZZZ.*

His smartphone on the desk vibrated with a violent, persistent hum.

A red banner flashed across the cracked screen—not a text message, not an incoming phone call, but an encrypted municipal emergency alert pushed directly through the city’s judicial network:

> **JUDICIAL WRIT: MANDATORY PSYCHIATRIC CONFINEMENT**  
> **CASE IDENTIFIER: HALLOWAY-VANE-009**  
> **AUTHORITY: MUNICIPAL COURT OF APPEALS / SECTION 12 EMERGENCY PROVISION**  
> **DIRECTIVE: IMMEDIATE APPREHENSION OF SUBJECT ELIAS VANE. AUTHORIZED USE OF NON-LETHAL NEURAL SUPPRESSION FORCE.**

"They're not waiting for morning," Elias whispered into his headset. "Halloway just filed an emergency psychiatric apprehension order."

"Look out your window, Elias," Kev snapped over the channel, his keyboard clattering frantically. "I just tapped into the traffic camera on your street corner. Two tactical vans just turned off the boulevard without their headlights."

Elias stood up. Through the rain-streaked window, he saw them—two matte-black vans rolling silently to the curb three stories below. 

"Pack your rig!" Viper roared into his ear. "Get the hell out of there!"

"I can't carry the triple-monitor rig down the fire escape," Elias said, his voice dropping into the cold, razor-sharp calm of an operator in the middle of a raid. 

He reached beneath the desk, pulled the master release levers on his central processing unit, and slid the solid-state storage core from its cradle. He shoved the drive, his burner phone, and a handful of cables into a waterproof nylon backpack.

He grabbed the heavy navy măng-tô coat from the back of the chair, swung it over his shoulders, and pulled the hood up.

"Lin, scrub my home IP address from the squad server," Elias commanded, walking swiftly toward the rear fire door. "Kev, set up a secure proxy bounce on the seaside relay."

"Where are you going, Vane?" Viper asked.

"To the only person who can help me take our minds back," Elias said, opening the metal fire door and stepping out into the pouring rain. "To Maren."
