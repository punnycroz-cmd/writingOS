# CHAPTER 31: NYX

The steel door wasn't broken down. It was cut.

Lena heard the torch before she saw it, a high, steady whine like an enormous insect. Then a red dot appeared on the inside of the door, right where the chain wrapped around the handle. The dot went from red to white and began to travel in a line, slowly, cutting through the thick steel like a knife through butter.

Viper had stopped convulsing. He lay in her lap breathing shallowly, eyes closed, his pulse coming down. A hundred and ten. A hundred. She laid his head on the floor with her jacket under it and rolled him onto his side into the recovery position.

"Stay right there," she said, though she knew he couldn't hear her. "Rosie still needs you."

She got up, went back to the console, and picked up the stun gun.

On the screen, the remaining three waveforms were still orange. Kev stable. Linh fast. And Elias... Elias's waveform was rising. Not by much. A little at a time, like a tide starting to come in, and it wouldn't stop. And her own fainter waveform, running alongside it, was rising with it.

In a corner of her head, the rubber band stretched. She could feel him hurting, a pain that had nothing to do with flesh, like a scream being held in.

*Viper,* she thought. *He just watched Viper go down.*

The torch had cut halfway around the door handle.

---

At 10:07, the door swung inward.

Four soldiers. Black ballistic helmets, night-vision goggles jutting out like insect eyes, short suppressed submachine guns. They came in in formation, two in front and two behind, the lights on their gun barrels sweeping the room.

Lena fired from behind the console. The electric round went out with a small *phut* and hit the lead man dead center in the chest, and she saw little blue sparks race down his body from chest to feet like snakes of light. He went down like a felled tree, rigid, his gun flying out of his hands.

She fired at the second one and missed. The round hit the steel doorframe and threw sparks.

The other three opened fire. It wasn't as loud as she'd imagined. Just dull *phut-phut-phut* sounds, like someone beating a rug. But the wooden table in front of her came apart in chunks, splinters flew into her face, the secondary monitor in the corner of the console exploded, and a round passed so close to her ear she could hear the wind of it.

She dropped flat behind the main console, the metal one. It would hold. She hoped it would hold.

"Dr. Solis." A man's voice came through a mask, frighteningly calm. "Put down your weapon. We don't want to hurt you. Our orders are to bring you back intact."

*Intact.* She knew exactly what that word meant. They needed her brain. The rest of her, they didn't.

Outside the concrete building a new sound started up, an electronic hum, deep and heavy, layer on layer, getting louder.

Through the cut steel door, Lena saw them. Three drones the size of small cars, glossy black, four rotors each, hovering right outside the door at head height. Under each one was a multi-barrel gun mount, slowly rotating. All three were aimed straight into the control room. At the console. At her. At the four folding chairs behind her.

Lena watched the barrels turn. She did the math. One three-second burst from those things and this room would be meat, and the four people asleep in those chairs would never know what had happened.

"Last time," the soldier said. "Put down your weapon."

Lena looked at the stun gun in her hands, at Elias's waveform on the screen, still climbing. She put the gun down on the floor.

---

The console's main screen went black. A deep black, nothing like the black of a dead screen. In the middle of it an image appeared, a black crescent moon on a purple background, and beneath it a line of white text:

> `Need a hand, Doc?`

Lena stared at the screen.

Outside the door, the hum of the three drones changed abruptly, rising and then dropping, like a piece of music changing key. The red lights under all three drones turned blue. Then, in perfect sync, as if choreographed, all three rotated a hundred and eighty degrees in midair, their gun mounts swinging outward, toward the four black trucks parked in a half-circle on the open ground.

And they fired.

Lena couldn't see much, only what showed through the gap in the door: streaks of light, a rapid string of explosions, the headlights of one truck shattering, then a second truck's, shouting, and then an orange fireball blooming up and lighting the whole point as one truck's gas tank was shot through.

The three soldiers in the room spun around. One of them yelled into his radio.

"Command! The drones! The drones are—"

He didn't get to finish. The night-vision goggles on his mask burst into a little spray of white sparks like a sparkler. He cried out, tore the mask off, and threw it on the floor. The second soldier's goggles blew, then the third's. Three soldiers stood in the middle of the control room, blind in the dark, their masks at their feet.

One of them raised his gun toward the console and pulled the trigger. Nothing happened. He pulled it again, then looked down at the gun. A small LED on the receiver was blinking red.

> `[WEAPON LOCKED — ECHO AUTHENTICATION FAILURE]`

"Smart guns," said the voice from the computer speaker. A real woman's voice, raspy, the rasp of a longtime smoker, with a little Western twang. "Linked to the Echo in your helmets so they know who their owner is. Halloway likes everything to talk to everything. So do I. Especially when it talks to me."

The three soldiers looked at each other in the dark. Then one turned and ran, and the other two ran after him. Their boots pounded across the gravel outside and faded into the sound of burning trucks and other men shouting.

The soldier she'd stunned was still lying rigid on the floor, staring at the ceiling.

Lena stood up from behind the console. Her legs were shaking so badly she had to hold on to the edge.

"You're Nyx," she said to the empty air.

"Thomas called me N.," said the voice on the speaker. "So did Corwin. I never liked *Nyx*. Some kid on a forum gave me that name back in '98, and it stuck."

"Where are you?"

"Seattle. In a room with six monitors and a real cat named Hamish, who's sitting on my keyboard right now, so if I typo anything, it's his fault." A pause. "I called a friend at the FBI. Their rapid response team is in a helicopter out of Portland. Fifteen minutes."

"Thank you."

"Don't thank me yet." Nyx's voice changed, losing some of its dryness. "Look at Vane's waveform, Doc."

Lena turned to the screen. Elias's orange waveform wasn't rising slowly anymore. It was climbing, the peaks shooting up sharp as saw teeth, and along the edge of the screen a number was turning red:

> `[INTRACRANIAL PRESSURE: 38 mmHg — OVER THRESHOLD]`

In a corner of her head, the rubber band was stretched almost to snapping.

"Something's happening in the fortress," Nyx said. "I can't see it. I can only see the traffic. Somebody just logged into the Mnemosyne server with top-level privileges. The kind only one person has."

Lena knew who it was before Nyx said the name.

"Halloway went in herself," Nyx said. "And your guy is standing right in front of her."

---

There was a sound at the door. Lena snatched the stun gun off the floor and raised it.

A man stood on the threshold of the cut steel door. Not in combat gear. A gray wool overcoat, graying hair, heavy bags under his eyes, both hands raised to shoulder height, palms open. Dr. Bradley.

"Don't shoot," he said, his voice tired. Not frightened. Just tired. "I'm not armed."

"Back up."

"I won't come in." He stood still and looked past her shoulder at the four folding chairs, at Elias. "He's hitting his limit, isn't he?"

Lena didn't answer.

"I was there that night," Bradley said. "2004. I was a level-two technician. I'm the one who found him sitting in front of that ventilation shaft. I'm the one who had to drag him away from it." He swallowed. "He didn't cry. Seven years old, and he didn't cry. He just kept saying the same thing over and over: *I promised. I promised.*"

Lena looked at him.

"I sat by his bed when he woke up after Layer 4," Bradley said. "I told him what he saw was just noise." He gave a dry laugh. "Twenty years I've worked for her, Doctor. Twenty years I kept telling myself that if I stayed, at least there'd be somebody who was careful."

Lena heard that and thought of her mother sitting on the edge of the bed, the coffee stain on the cuff of her lab coat. *So I'm staying here. To make sure someone is careful.*

"She won't stop," Bradley said. "She's never trusted anybody else to do this. Never. She'll pull him into Layer 4 herself. And through this cable..." He looked at the red cable running from Elias's head to the console. The anchor line. "...she'll pull you in with him."

He lowered his hands slowly, so she could see he didn't mean to do anything, and sat down right there on the threshold with his back against the steel frame, like a man who had walked a very long way and couldn't walk any farther.

"I'll sit here," he said. "Until the FBI comes. I'll tell them everything."

Lena looked at him one second longer, then turned her back on him. She didn't have time for him anymore.

On the screen, Elias's waveform had hit the top red line, and her own fainter waveform, running beside it, was being dragged up after it.

"Nyx," she said. "How long until SWAT gets here?"

"Twelve minutes."

Lena looked at the clock on the wall, then at the numbers on the screen.

"He doesn't have twelve minutes," she said.

In Seattle, in a room with six monitors and a cat named Hamish, Nyx didn't answer.
