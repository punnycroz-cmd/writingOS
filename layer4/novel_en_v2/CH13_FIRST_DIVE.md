# CHAPTER 13: FIRST DIVE

"Ten minutes," Lena said. "I'll be counting down out here. You'll hear my voice the whole time. If I say *up*, you come up. No arguing."

"No arguing," Elias repeated.

He was sitting in the old armchair in Viper's living room, the only chair wide enough for him to lean back in. The curtains were drawn. Rosa had taken Rosie to the park. Viper sat at the dining table pretending to read the paper without taking his eyes off the living room, and Kev and Linh were in the study watching the outside connections.

Lena knelt beside the chair with the field kit open next to her. Nested in the foam were a portable scanner the size of a thick book, a folding sensor ring, some cooling patches, and three amber glass vials of stabilizer. She set the ring on his head and turned the knob, and the six probes seated against bone with a soft click.

"What am I looking for?" he asked.

"Nothing." She stuck an electrode to the inside of his wrist. "That's the mistake people make. They dive to find something specific, and their brain builds exactly that thing for them to see. That's how false memories get made. Nobody has to plant them." She checked the screen. "Just sink and look. Whatever comes up, look at it. Don't try to hold on, and don't go searching."

"Like watching a movie."

"Like sitting at a bus station watching people go by." She put her hand on the switch. "Close your eyes. In for four, hold for two, out for six." Her voice slowed and dropped. "Layer 2 in three, two, one."

---

Layer 2 was nothing like Layer 1. Layer 1 had ground and sky and rules. Layer 2 had none of that. It was like sinking into a warm, dark lake where images drifted by like glowing fish, slow, with no before and no after.

He saw a lime-yellow truck, a unicorn blanket, a goldfish swimming sideways. Memories from the last few days, shallow and close, still warm.

"You're in the shallows," Lena's voice called down from far above, like someone calling down a well. "Let it take you down. Don't swim."

He let himself sink. The water got darker, colder. The glowing fish thinned out and disappeared. There was a long, empty stretch of dark, exactly like the blankness he felt whenever he tried to remember his life.

Then there was light at the bottom. White fluorescent light, flickering.

He was standing in a hallway. Pale green linoleum, a crack running diagonally from the base of the left wall, white-painted walls scuffed and smudged at knee height like children's handprints, the smell of bleach and boiled cabbage drifting in from somewhere. He had drawn this hallway thousands of times, and now he was standing in it.

More precisely, he was looking at it from much lower down, from a child's eye level. He looked down and saw two tiny feet in gray cloth slippers, white cotton clothes with a *0* printed on the chest, two skinny hands, a bruise in the crook of one elbow where they drew blood. Seven years old. Maybe only six.

Someone was walking him along. A big hand in a latex glove rested on his shoulder and pushed him gently forward. He couldn't see the person's face, only a white lab coat and an ID badge swinging right in front of his eyes: *PROMETHEUS — TECHNICIAN — LEVEL 2*. They stopped in front of door *0*. The person opened it and pushed him inside.

A small room with an iron bed, a desk bolted to the wall, a little window high up with frosted glass that showed nothing outside. On the desk sat a box of crayons and a stack of white paper. The door shut behind him, and then the lock turned.

The child he was sat down on the edge of the bed, hands on his thighs, looking at the opposite wall. Elias could feel what the child felt, and what surprised him was that it wasn't fear. Fear was old, worn out long ago. All that was left was a tired emptiness, and waiting.

Waiting for what?

The child got up, went to the left wall, pressed his palm against it, then made a fist and knocked. Three quick, *tok tok tok*. Two slow, *tok, tok*. Then he stood still and listened.

From the other side of the wall, very faintly, came three quick, two slow.

The child's chest burst open. A rush of warmth flooded his whole body, so strong that Elias, in a grown man's body sitting twenty years away, felt it like a punch to the middle of his chest.

*She's here.*

The child knocked again, and the other side answered. The child knocked another sequence, longer, more complicated: two quick, one slow, four quick. The other side paused, then answered with a different sequence. It was a conversation, in a language only two people knew. Elias couldn't understand the long sequences. They lived on a deeper level, a blurrier one. But the first one, the shortest one, he understood. He understood it with his body, not his head.

*Three quick, two slow. I'm here.*

"Elias," Lena's voice called down. "Four minutes left. Where are you?"

He wanted to answer, but he didn't want to leave. He kept the child's small hand against the wall and listened.

On the other side of the wall there was singing. Very soft, a woman's low, rough voice, singing a lullaby in a foreign language, coming through the wall from somewhere farther off, like the room beyond that one. The same lullaby he had heard in Lena's clinic, the one that had made her cry.

The child slid down and sat on the floor at the foot of the wall, his back against it, eyes closed, listening. And from the other side of the wall, also at knee height, came a faint rustling, as if someone had just sat down too and leaned against the very same spot.

Two children sitting on either side of a wall, back to back through the bricks, listening to the same song.

Elias felt his eyes burn. His real eyes, the eyes of the twenty-seven-year-old man sitting in the old armchair in Viper's house.

"Elias, your heart rate's climbing," Lena's voice was closer now. "Two minutes. I'm starting to bring you up."

*Wait. Just a little longer.*

In the memory the lullaby had ended, and the child was still sitting there. The knocking came again from the other side, lower this time, near the floor, as if someone were knocking with a heel. Three quick, two slow.

And right then, in the real world, Elias heard it. Not in the memory but outside, right next to him, right against the arm of the chair: three quick, two slow, light and even, like fingers on fabric.

The two sounds layered over each other, one in the memory and one outside, the same rhythm, the same pauses, fitting together like the two halves of something snapped in two.

The white hallway shook. The fluorescent lights flickered wildly. The wall at the child's back dissolved like sand.

"Up. Up now, Elias."

---

He opened his eyes. Viper's living room, curtains drawn, dim yellow afternoon light slipping through the gap, the case open beside him, the scanner screen blinking orange numbers. Lena was kneeling by the chair with a vial of stabilizer ready in one hand, the other resting on the arm of the chair right next to his.

The fingers of that hand were tapping. Three quick, two slow. She wasn't looking at her hand. She was busy watching the screen, reading the numbers, her lips moving as she counted. She didn't know she was tapping. She had never known. If anyone asked, she'd say it was a habit from when she was little.

Elias stared at those fingers.

"No bleeding," Lena said, her eyes still on the screen. "Heart rate's down, intracranial pressure's fine. Good. Much better than I expected." She let out a breath, set down the injector, and only then turned to look at him. "What did you see?"

He didn't answer. He was still looking at her hand. Lena followed his gaze down to her own fingers, stopped in the middle of a beat on the worn fabric of the armrest.

"What is it?" she asked.

Slowly, as though afraid of breaking something, Elias lifted his own hand and set it on the armrest beside hers, a couple of inches away, and knocked. Three quick, two slow.

Lena froze.

At the dining table, Viper put down his newspaper. In the study doorway, Linh's head poked out. No one said a word.

"Three quick, two slow," Elias said, his voice thick, like a man who had just been crying, though his eyes were dry. "I just heard it. In the memory, through a wall. Two kids, each in their own room, knocking for each other every night."

Lena looked at his hand, then at her own.

"What does it mean?" she asked, very quietly. She already knew. He could see in her eyes that she knew. But she needed to hear it out loud.

"It means *I'm here*," Elias said.
