# CHAPTER 30: VIPER

Marcus Reyes had never punched anyone.

Not in school, when Kyle Dunbar called him Marcy all through fourth grade. Not at twenty-two, when the owner of the first bakery he worked at stiffed him three months' pay. Not that night at the hospital, when the surgeon came out of the OR and said a sentence that started with *we did everything we could*, and he stood in the middle of the hallway with his fists clenched so hard his nails cut into his palms, and there was nothing to punch.

He was a big man, so everyone figured he got into fights. People always figure big men get into fights. Really, all he knew how to do was bake.

And now here he was, standing in the middle of a plaza paved with black stone, under a rust-red sky, a gun bigger than he was on his shoulder, yelling at a fortress.

It felt great.

---

For the first twelve minutes, everything went exactly the way Vane said it would. He yelled, he fired, and the red lights on the walls turned toward him like moths to a flame. Then the soldiers in black started pouring out of small doors at the base of the walls, running at him with their glowing red rifles.

He shot them. The railgun went off like thunder, and the lead soldier burst into a cloud of pixel dust. Three seconds later he reappeared in the same spot, looking lost. Viper shot him again.

"Viper, your left," Kev said in his ear.

He turned left and fired. Another one gone.

"Viper, behind you."

He didn't turn around. He heard a small crack from the black glass tower in the distance, and the soldier behind him vanished before he could pull the trigger.

"Thanks, Kev."

"Don't thank me. Shoot."

He shot. He yelled. He shot again.

At one point, between explosions, he thought about Rosie. Not worried, just the way you think about someone while you're doing something silly and fun: *I wish she could see her dad right now.* He figured she'd love it. She'd clap and tell him he was a superhero. Then he thought, no, she'd probably tell him he was yelling too loud and hurting her ears.

He laughed out loud in the middle of the plaza, in the middle of the red gunfire.

---

"We've got a problem," Kev said.

Viper was reloading. It had been twenty-eight minutes in here, seventeen seconds outside. He was starting to feel tired. His arms in here didn't know how to get tired. The tiredness was somewhere deeper, like the days he stood at the oven for twelve hours straight at Christmas.

"What?"

"A turret just came up on top of the main gate." Kev's voice was even, but faster than usual. "Three barrels, locking onto Elias and Lin. I can't hit it from here. The angle's wrong."

He straightened up. "Where are they?"

"Inner courtyard, behind the third wall, two hundred yards from the gate." A pause. "There's something else down there too. A lot of them. Some kind of... I don't know. Echoes. Hundreds."

"Echoes fight?"

"Not yet. They're just standing there." Kev paused. "Viper, that turret takes ten seconds to charge, and then it fires. Elias can dodge it. Lin can't."

Viper looked toward the fortress. Three walls, each higher than the last, soldiers in black still pouring out of every door.

"How long for me to get there?"

"Going Vane's way? Four minutes."

"Straight through?"

A long silence.

"Viper," Kev said. "Straight through means through three walls and twenty soldiers."

"How long?"

"...Forty seconds. If you don't stop."

Viper looked at the railgun on his shoulder, then down at his scratched gunmetal titanium armor, at the pink floral apron still tied around his waist, the apron Rosie had made him wear in game two years ago because *you look more like a cook than a soldier, and nobody shoots a cook.*

"Kev," he said. "How many hits do you think this armor can take?"

"Viper—"

"Answer me."

"...Enough to get you to the gate. Not enough to get you back."

Viper nodded. He'd figured as much.

"Tell Lin I said run," he said. "Tell Vane... never mind. He knows."

Then he ran.

---

He went straight into the first wall. Not over it. Into it. He aimed the railgun dead ahead and fired at the base of the wall from ten feet away. The recoil shoved him back, but the black stone cracked open into a hole the size of a doorway, and he charged through before the dust settled.

On the other side were two soldiers. They turned and raised their rifles. He didn't shoot. He charged, slammed his shoulder into the first one's chest, and sent him flying like a rag doll. The second one fired, and a red round hit his chest plate. It didn't hurt. It just landed heavy, like someone had thrown a sack of flour at him. In the corner of his vision, a green bar dropped by a fifth.

He kept running.

The second wall. Fire, crack, through. Four of them. He shot two, rammed one, and the fourth put three rounds into his back before Kev dropped him from a distance. The green bar was at half and turning yellow.

"Viper, twenty seconds," Kev said.

The third wall, the highest and the thickest. He fired, and the wall cracked but didn't break. He fired again, and the hole was only big enough for a dog. He dropped the railgun, backed up five steps, then ran and threw all six and a half feet of armored body into the crack. The wall gave way. He tumbled through the hole, rolled across the black stone courtyard, and when he looked up, he saw them.

Vane was standing in the middle of the courtyard with both blue blades drawn, in front of Linh. She stood behind him, tiny, hugging the diamond-shaped decoder to her chest. All around them stood hundreds of black animals, perfectly still, their eyes dull silver.

And on top of the gate, the three-barreled turret was glowing red, the energy rings at the ends of the barrels spinning faster and faster.

"Three seconds," Kev said.

Viper got up.

He wasn't thinking anything. That was the strangest part. His whole life he'd thought too much. About rent, about Rosie's school fees, about his wife's headaches that he hadn't noticed in time. Right now his head was empty. There was only a distance, sixty feet between him and where Vane was standing, and a number. Three seconds.

He ran. Sixty feet. Forty-five. Thirty.

Vane turned and saw him, and his eyes went wide.

The turret fired.

Three red beams as thick as tree trunks speared down into the courtyard. Viper jumped. Not very high. Just high enough for his big body to get in the way of all three beams, between the barrels and his two friends.

This time it wasn't a sack of flour. It was a whole wall. He felt the titanium armor split open across his chest, his shoulders, his stomach. He felt the pink apron burn to ash. The yellow bar in the corner of his vision went red, blinked, and disappeared.

He fell onto the stone and lay on his back, looking up at the red sky with its characters falling like snow. It didn't hurt. He just felt heavy, like sinking into a bed that was much too soft.

A face appeared above him. Vane, white as a sheet.

"Viper—"

"Gate," he said, his voice crackling like it was coming out of a broken speaker. "Go. Takes ten seconds to recharge."

"You—"

"Go, you idiot. I didn't go down so you could stand here and stare at me."

Another face. Linh. She was crying, virtual tears running down her pearl-blue skin.

"Viper," she said. "Viper, please, don't."

"Lin." He lifted one hand, heavy as lead, and laid it on her head and ruffled her hair, the way he ruffled Rosie's every night before he turned out the light. "Your bánh mì's better than my chili. Don't tell anybody."

She made a sound, half laugh, half sob.

He saw the edges of his vision start to break apart, pixels peeling off at the rim and floating up like ash rising from a fire. He looked at Vane one last time.

"Tell Rosie," he said, "her dad won."

Then the red sky was gone.

---

Lena was standing behind the console with the stun gun aimed at the steel door, which was still shaking after the second explosion, when the third chair began to shake too.

She turned. Viper was convulsing. His whole body arched off the back of the chair, his hands clawed, his head thrown back, his mouth wide open. On the screen, his orange waveform spiked straight up, then dropped straight down, and a line of red text began to blink:

> `[VIPER_99: FORCED DISCONNECT — FEEDBACK SHOCK DETECTED]`

The steel door shook a third time. The chain rattled.

Lena looked at the door, then at Viper. She set the stun gun on the console, still aimed at the door, ran to the third chair, and knelt. She held Viper's head so it wouldn't hit the frame of the chair, pulled the sensor ring off his head, and pressed two fingers to his neck. His pulse was fast, very fast, but it was there.

"Marcus," she said. "Marcus, can you hear me?"

He didn't answer. His eyes were open, seeing nothing.

Behind her, the steel door let out a long metallic groan, and she heard the chain begin to give, one link at a time.

She didn't turn around. She held Viper's head in her lap, counted his pulse, and waited.
