# CHAPTER 12: FACE TO FACE

For three years Nguyễn Hoài Linh had pictured this moment more times than she could count, and not once, in all that picturing, had it gone like this.

In her head, the team met up at a gaming convention: blazing lights, thumping music, a packed crowd. She would walk in, a little taller thanks to platform sneakers, her hair dyed blue like her avatar's, and drop some line that was extremely cool. Viper would hug her, Kev would nod, and Eli would... honestly, she had never been able to picture what Eli would do. Probably stand in a corner and watch.

In reality she was riding shotgun in a beat-up gray pickup next to a skinny thirty-year-old in black-framed glasses with his windbreaker zipped up to his chin, who had been driving for four hours and had said exactly eleven sentences. She'd counted.

"Kev," she asked for the third time, "are you seriously this quiet in real life?"

Kev glanced at her and went back to the road. "Yes."

Twelve sentences.

"You talk so much in game, though. You sing. That time we won the semifinal you sang the entire Canadian national anthem."

"I'm not Canadian."

"I know! That's what makes it funny!"

Kev thought about it for a while. "In game nobody's looking at my face," he said. "It's easier."

Linh looked at him: at the way his hands sat on the wheel at exactly ten and two, at the way he checked the rearview mirror every nine seconds, regular as a clock, at the way he flinched, very slightly, whenever a truck passed on the left. She knew Kev had been in the military. He never talked about it much. Only once, at three in the morning after a loss, he'd let it slip: *I lived two years in one week once. Wouldn't recommend it.*

She looked out the window and said, "I'm the opposite. I talk twice as much in real life."

The corner of Kev's mouth twitched up. "I know."

Fourteen sentences, plus a smile. She decided to count it as a win.

---

They reached Viper's house at ten in the morning. Linh jumped out before Kev could cut the engine and stood in the middle of the lawn taking it in: the sky-blue wooden house, the tire swing, the lime-yellow truck in the driveway with the sombrero crow on its side.

The front door opened, and a huge man stepped onto the porch holding a spatula.

"Viper?" Linh said.

The man looked at her, looked down, then looked down a little more.

"Lin?" Viper said, like he couldn't believe it.

"Got a problem?"

"You're..." Viper hunted for the word. "You're tiny."

Linh's avatar was six feet tall. In real life she was five feet even, in shoes.

"You're fatter than in game," Linh shot back instantly.

"That's armor!"

"That's a belly!"

Viper opened his mouth, shut it, then exploded into laughter like a thunderclap. He tossed the spatula onto the porch rail, came down the steps, and hoisted her up like a sack of rice.

"Put me down! You giant! Virtual or real, you're going to break my ribs!"

Kev shut the truck door and stood at the edge of the yard with his hands in his windbreaker pockets. Viper set Linh down and turned to him. The two men looked at each other. Three years, hundreds of matches, who knew how many sleepless nights on the same voice channel, and now they stood fifteen feet apart on a lawn strewn with kids' toys, with no idea what to do with their hands.

Viper held out his hand. Kev took it. They shook once, stiffly, and let go.

"Cheese puffs," Viper said.

"It was my mouse," Kev said.

Then Viper pulled him into a hug. Kev stood rigid as a plank for three seconds, then patted Viper's back twice with one hand, about as awkwardly as it could possibly be done.

Linh turned away and pretended to look at a squirrel in the oak tree, because her eyes were stinging and she would rather die than admit it.

Then she saw him.

Eli was standing in the doorway with one hand on the frame. Thinner than she'd thought, taller than she'd thought, messy black hair, gray eyes, wearing a huge T-shirt that had to be Viper's. He was watching her, watching Kev, watching Viper, with the look of someone standing outside a bakery window looking in.

Beside him, a little behind, stood a woman with dark hair in a loose knot, dark eyes, a slightly pointed chin. Linh had seen that face before, three nights ago, floating over the table in the Crow's Nest, drawn out of Eli's memory.

She walked to the bottom of the porch steps, looked up at Eli, then at the woman.

"You're the person in his head," Linh said.

The woman looked a little startled, then nodded. "I guess I am."

"I'm Linh. Lin, in game. I'm the one who ran the search that came up empty." She held out her hand. "Sorry I blew it."

The woman looked at her hand, then shook it. Her hand was cold, but her grip was firm.

"I'm Lena," she said. "It wasn't your fault. That name was wiped out of every database twenty years ago."

Linh didn't understand what that meant. But she saw the way Eli looked at the woman while she said it, and she decided she was going to like her.

---

At three that afternoon, all five of them logged into the Crow's Nest at once.

Viper had set up a local network in the study with five headsets: two of his, one Kev had brought, one of Linh's, and an ancient one Viper had dug out of the garage, with duct tape wrapped around its titanium ring. Kev spent two hours routing the connection through four layers of relay servers scattered from Iceland to Singapore so nobody could trace it.

"Fifteen minutes," Kev said once everyone was in position. "No more. Any session longer than fifteen minutes leaves enough of a trail for someone to analyze."

Lena was the last to put on her headset. Linh watched her hold it for a long time, turning the titanium ring over in her hands like a scientist examining a specimen.

"Have you never been in Layer 1?" Linh asked.

"Sure, for work. To examine memories created in there." She paused. "But never to... play."

Linh looked at her, then at Eli in the next chair, who was looking at her the exact same way.

"Then brace yourself," Linh said, and closed her eyes.

---

Lena opened her eyes on a wooden step. Pine resin, rain, a hint of burned popcorn. A light, cool breeze moved through her hair, carrying the tinkle of wind chimes somewhere high above.

She looked up and saw a spaceship growing out of the top of a tree.

She had seen hundreds of Layer 1 spaces: glossy, soulless corporate offices, conference rooms, therapy rooms, meditation gardens designed down to the last leaf by teams of experts. She had never seen anything like this.

It was a mess. It made no sense. A rusted steel hull draped across three ancient branches, paper lanterns in every color strung along crooked plank walkways, a red slide curling around the trunk. On the bow, the huge black crow was winking beside *NO GROWN-UPS. EXCEPT VIPER.*, and underneath, someone had added in a different color of paint: *AND THE FISH DOCTOR.*

"Rosie made me add that this morning," Viper's voice said behind her. His avatar was enormous in its titanium armor, still wearing the pink apron. "She's got sub-admin rights. Don't ask me why."

Lena didn't know whether to laugh or cry, so she did a little of both.

Linh, now a pearl-skinned girl with pointed ears, took her hand and pulled her along. "Come on, I'll give you the tour."

They went all over the Crow's Nest, Linh pointing out everything, talking without a break. Bubbles, Linh's snow-white Echo cat, trotted along behind them, and every time Linh laughed it blew out a string of soap bubbles. Brisket was sprawled across the kitchen doorway, and Kev's paper crane sat motionless on the bow like a sentry.

"Where's your Echo?" Linh asked.

"I turned mine off five years ago," Lena said. "My job is checking whether memories have been tampered with. I can't do that with something Halloway wrote sitting on my shoulder listening to everything I say." She paused. "Corwin turned his off too. He said it was the one thing he learned from his father. Never let a machine know what you're thinking."

Linh looked at her, then nodded, unusually serious. "Kev says the exact same thing."

This was the command room, where the team planned tactics before every match, with a round table made from a ship's wheel and a star map on the ceiling that Kev had painted himself, star by star, matching the night sky on his mother's birthday. This was Viper's kitchen, where nobody could actually eat anything but he cooked anyway, because *smell is what matters most*. And this was Linh's room, crammed with odd machines that did nothing except make happy noises and release soap bubbles.

"What do you build these for?" Lena asked, looking at a cat-shaped machine blowing bubbles out of its ears.

"Nothing." Linh shrugged. "Out there, everything I do has to have a reason. Pass the exam, get a job, pay rent, send money home to my parents in Saigon. In here I get to make useless stuff. That's the whole point."

Lena watched the cat machine. A big bubble floated up, shimmering, and burst into tiny stars.

For five years she had been examining things made in Layer 1, and she had always thought of it as a tool, a work environment, a scene that might contain evidence. It had never occurred to her that it could be a home.

---

They met in the command room with ten minutes left.

"Here's the plan," Kev said, projecting a diagram onto the tabletop. "We've got Elias's drive with the original Prometheus archive, but that's only part of it. Halloway's current system, where all the Mnemosyne data lives, is somewhere on a Layer 3 server. We don't know where."

"Elias's brain knows," Lena said.

Everyone turned to look at her.

"When Layer 4 broke, his brain pulled in a lot of things," she explained. "Corwin's memories, pieces of Prometheus, maybe even connection traces from that system itself. If I guide him into Layer 2, a little at a time, safely, under control, we might be able to recover those pieces. His memories and the path to the server."

"Is it dangerous?" Viper asked.

"Yes," Lena said. She didn't lie. "But less dangerous than sitting here doing nothing."

Elias, silent until now, nodded. "I'll do it."

"No more than ten minutes at a time," Lena said. "With me monitoring from outside, with the stabilizer ready. If I say stop—"

"I stop."

Kev made a note on the diagram. "We start tomorrow. Time's up. Everybody out."

One by one they got up. Linh dragged Viper off first, the two of them arguing about a popcorn recipe. Kev vanished in a puff of gray smoke.

Lena lingered a little. She wanted to look at the Crow's Nest one more time before she left. She walked down the corridor inside the hull, past doors with names chalked on them: *Viper — DO NOT ENTER WHILE COOKING*, *Lin — DANGER — BUBBLES*. Kev's door had no writing at all, only a paper crane taped to it.

The last door had no name and stood ajar. Lena pushed it open and went in.

It was a small, bare room. No strange machines, no lanterns, just one chair, one window looking out over the sea of clouds, and four walls. But the walls weren't white. They were covered in drawings, hundreds, maybe thousands of pencil drawings layered over each other from floor to ceiling, and all of them showed the same thing, again and again, from every angle: a hallway, a floor with a diagonal crack, fluorescent lights overhead, and at the end of the hall, two white doors set right next to each other. One marked *0*, the other *1*.

Lena stood frozen in the doorway, her heart pounding.

"He draws it all the time," Linh's voice said behind her, very softly. She had come back at some point without Lena hearing. "Every time he loses a match, every time he can't sleep, he comes in here and draws. Three years. We asked what it was, and he said he didn't know. His hand just draws it."

Lena walked into the room and put her hand on the wall, on a drawing of door number *1* where the charcoal had smeared from being drawn over so many times.

Elias had drawn this door thousands of times without knowing why, without knowing who was behind it. Behind that door was her.
