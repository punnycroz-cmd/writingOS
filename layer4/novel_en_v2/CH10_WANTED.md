# CHAPTER 10: WANTED

They spent the rest of the night in a twenty-four-hour laundromat four blocks from the overpass.

It was Lena's idea. "At three in the morning nobody notices who's sitting in a laundromat," she said. "Everyone's got their own reason for not sleeping." She fed a few quarters into an empty dryer so it would tumble and roar, then sat down on a plastic chair beside it with the case between her feet and his coat still around her shoulders.

Elias sat across from her with his back to the wall, watching the glass door. His left hand was wrapped in gauze from her case, and blood had soaked through it in a brown stain. Under the flickering fluorescent lights of the laundromat she had put two stitches in his palm, no anesthetic, quick and neat, and he hadn't made a sound.

"Doesn't that hurt?" she'd asked as she tied off the thread.

"Sure."

"You're not reacting at all."

"I guess someone taught me not to react," he said. "A long time ago."

She looked at him for a moment, then snipped the thread and said nothing more.

Now she was asleep, her head tipped onto her shoulder, sitting upright on the plastic chair like someone dozing on a bus. Elias watched her for a little while, then looked back out at the street. The rain had thinned. A garbage truck went by. A stray cat crossed the sidewalk.

He took out his phone, the old one with the cracked screen, the only thing he'd brought with him from his previous life. He knew it might be tracked. He knew that was why Lena had thrown hers into a puddle. But it was also the only line connecting him to the three people in the world who might be able to help him.

He opened the crow app.

> **Elias:** Something happened.

This time there were no three dots. The phone buzzed almost immediately with a voice call.

"Do you know what time it is?" Kev's voice, wide awake. He obviously hadn't been asleep. "I've got the city police channel on. There's a fire at the St. Jude Medical Building, fourth floor, suite 4B."

"I know. I was there when it started."

Silence on the line for a moment. "Is she okay?"

Elias looked at Lena asleep. "She's okay."

"What phone are you on?"

"My old one."

"When we hang up, turn it off, pull the SIM, and snap it in half." Kev's voice was even and unhurried, like he was reading off a checklist. "The Crow's Nest app runs through Lin's private server, so I think that part's safe, but I can't vouch for the handset. An hour ago someone logged into your Somnia account from an address in Halloway's IP range. They're going through your login history to map your contacts."

"Looking for you guys."

"Looking for us," Kev corrected. "You need somewhere to hide. Not a hotel. Not a motel. Somewhere with no record of your name, no record of hers, and no cameras."

"I don't know anywhere like that."

"I do," another voice cut in, deep and groggy but waking up word by word. Viper. Kev had pulled him into the call at some point. "Where are you?"

Elias read off the street name printed on the laundromat's sign.

"Two hours," Viper said. "No, an hour forty if I don't stop for gas. Stay put. Don't let anybody see your face."

"Viper, you've got a kid—"

"Rosie's asleep. Her grandma's right next door. I left a note." Rustling, a ring of keys jingling. "Oh, and heads up. My ride isn't exactly subtle."

---

Dawn came up gray through the laundromat window. At six, Lena jolted awake, her hand already on the handle of the case before her eyes were open, and only when she saw Elias still sitting there and the laundromat still empty did her shoulders come down.

On the opposite wall was a TV on a bracket, the kind laundromats leave on the weather channel for people waiting on their loads. At some point someone had turned it on, muted, picture only. The morning news, a blond anchor, then footage of a brick building pouring smoke with fire trucks parked below, and a crawl across the bottom of the screen: *FIRE AT ST. JUDE MEDICAL BUILDING — SUSPECT AT LARGE.*

Then a face came up. His face.

It was a photo from the hospital, taken right after he woke up: skin dead white, eyes glassy, hair plastered down. He looked like something that had just crawled out of a grave. Under it was his name, his age, and a few words: *Dangerous psychiatric patient. May be armed.*

Elias got up, went over to the TV, and found the volume button.

"...the fire broke out at the office of Dr. Lena Hale, a memory forensics specialist. According to police sources, the suspect is Elias Vane, twenty-seven, a patient who recently left Halloway Medical Center against medical advice after being diagnosed with acute delusional disorder. Dr. Hale's whereabouts are unknown. Police believe she was taken by the suspect..."

The screen cut to an overhead clip, security-camera style, washed out and grainy: a dark alley, a tall man dragging a woman along by the wrist while she struggled, looked back, her mouth open as if screaming for help.

Elias watched it. That was the alley behind St. Jude, that was his coat, that was Lena's hair. But he hadn't dragged her anywhere. She had run ahead of him.

"That's fake," Lena said behind him.

She had stood up and come to his side, her eyes locked on the screen, an examiner at work.

"Look at the woman's shadow," she said. "The streetlight's on the left, so the man's shadow falls to the right. That's correct. Hers falls to the right too, but it's off by about ten degrees, like it's lit by a different source. And look at the hairline here." She pointed at the screen. "Every time she turns her head, the edge of her hair blurs for one frame before it sharpens again. That's face-swap software. They got a picture of me somewhere, mapped it onto an actress, and staged the whole thing."

"In one night?"

"In one hour, with enough hardware." She laughed, dry. "Last year I testified in two face-swap cases. Both times the jury didn't believe me. People believe what they see."

On the TV, the anchor was still talking. "...police are advising the public not to approach the suspect. Anyone with information should call the hotline..."

Elias turned the TV off. He stood looking at the black screen, at his own dim reflection in the glass. A lunatic who kidnapped a woman, an arsonist. That was the man the whole city was looking for, and that was the man they'd see whenever they looked at him.

"They don't need to catch you," Lena said quietly. "They just need nobody to believe you anymore."

---

At 6:40, Viper arrived.

They heard him before they saw him: a horn, but not a normal horn. It was a tune, seven bright, cheerful, weirdly familiar notes ringing out down the still-sleepy street. Lena looked at Elias. Elias looked at Lena.

A small lime-yellow truck swung into the curb outside the laundromat and stopped with a squeal of brakes. Painted on its side was a black crow in a wide-brimmed sombrero holding an enormous taco, and above it, in curly red letters: *CROW TACO — SO GOOD YOU'LL CAW FOR MORE!* On the roof, a glowing plastic taco sign was slowly rotating.

The door popped open and a big man climbed out, close to six-five, broad shoulders, a bit of a gut, a thick beard, a scar running through his left eyebrow. He wore a black T-shirt that said *WORLD'S BEST DAD* and a backward baseball cap, and he had a paper cup of coffee in each hand.

He pushed open the laundromat door, looked around, and saw them.

"Vane," Viper said, and looked Elias over from head to foot, a long, careful look, as if comparing the skinny man in front of him with some picture in his head. Then he laughed, loud and short. "Damn, you're skinnier than I thought." He held out a coffee. "Three years, and I finally get to see your actual face."

Elias took the coffee and didn't know what to say. This man had driven almost two hours in the middle of the night, leaving his eight-year-old daughter asleep, to pick up someone he had never met in person, someone who couldn't even remember who he was.

"Thank you," he said. It was much too small.

"Hold the thanks." Viper turned to Lena, took off his cap, and gave a little bow, an old-fashioned gesture that looked almost funny on a man his size. "Morning, Dr. Hale. I'm Marcus. My friends call me Viper. I'm real sorry about your office."

Lena looked at him, then at the lime-yellow truck with the sombrero crow outside the window, at the taco sign turning on the roof. Her lips trembled, and Elias thought she was about to cry, but what came out was a laugh, small and short, close to panic, but a real laugh.

"This is your getaway car?" she asked.

"Nobody goes looking for a crazy kidnapper in a taco truck," Viper said seriously. "Trust me. It's the perfect cover."

"It plays music."

"Horn broke last year. Haven't had the money to fix it." Viper shrugged. "Anyway, my Rosie loves it."

---

Lena sat between the boxes of onions.

The back of the truck was cramped and thick with the smell of spices: cumin, dried chiles, onion, lime. There was a fold-down steel stove against one wall, a small refrigerator humming, racks of pots and pans that clanked every time the truck took a corner. Viper had cleared them a spot between two stacks of cardboard boxes marked *ONIONS — 50 LB* and spread a pink unicorn blanket on the floor.

"That's Rosie's," Viper called back through the little hatch to the cab. "She made me bring it. Said kidnapped people need a unicorn blanket."

Elias sat across from Lena with his back against the refrigerator. Through the rear window the city slid away, gray and wet, thinning out, giving way to fields, wooden fences, tin-roofed barns. No one was following them. There was no black sedan in the mirrors.

Viper turned on the radio, an old country song, and sang along, wrong words, rough voice, drumming his fingers on the steering wheel off the beat. Elias looked out the window for a while, then back at Lena.

She was sitting with her knees pulled up on the unicorn blanket, the case wedged against her hip, a smear of soot on one cheek she'd forgotten to wipe off. She was watching Viper through the little hatch, and the corners of her mouth had turned up a little.

Viper went sharp on a high note, and a pot fell off the rack and hit the floor with a *clang*.

Elias laughed. He didn't mean to. It just came out of somewhere in his chest he hadn't known was there. A short, hoarse laugh, clumsy, like a machine that hadn't run in a long time, but real.

Lena turned and looked at him. For the first time since he'd walked into 4B, soaking wet, calling her by a stranger's name, she looked at him with her guard all the way down. Only for a second. Then she turned away and pulled the unicorn blanket up to her shoulders.

But Elias had seen it.
