# CHAPTER 8: THE FILE IN THE WALL

The St. Jude Building shut off its hallway lights at nine. By ten, the only sounds were the hum of the heating and the rain on the roof.

Lena drew the office curtains, plugged Elias's hard drive into her main machine through an isolated port, and cut every network connection in the room. She worked fast and clean without a word, her fingers moving over the keyboard with the rhythm of someone who had done this a thousand times.

Elias stood behind her, leaning against the bookshelf. He watched the screen, but mostly he watched her: the way she tilted her head when she read a line of code, the way she bit her lower lip whenever a command failed, the way her shoulders went rigid every time a car passed outside. She hadn't told him about the nursing home. He knew something had happened, because yesterday she'd come back later than she said, with red eyes, but he didn't ask. Rule number one: this was an investigation.

"Nyx's key fits the outer shell," Lena said. "It's decompressing. Most of it's junk. Connection logs, calibration settings from the five prep sessions. But there's a partition at the very bottom that doesn't belong to any session." She frowned. "Like someone slipped it onto your machine when you weren't looking. Or it followed you out of the network."

"When everything shattered, things stuck to me," Elias said. "Corwin's memories. Maybe other things too."

The progress bar crawled to ninety percent, ninety-four, ninety-nine. The screen went gray and a document opened.

> `[ORIGINAL ARCHIVE — PROMETHEUS INSTITUTE FOR BIOLOGICAL COGNITION]`
> `[CLASSIFICATION: LEVEL 0]`
> `[CREATED: 10/15/2004 — 06:12]`

Lena stopped typing. Elias pushed off from the bookshelf and came closer.

```
==================================================================
INCIDENT REPORT — DIVISION 4 (EAST HARBOR SITE)
==================================================================
Time: 10/14/2004, 02:40 – 03:55
Classification: Deliberate internal sabotage

SEQUENCE OF EVENTS:
Biochemistry Lead Elena Solis disabled isolation-wing security at
02:40, opened the holding rooms of the core paired subjects (Zero
and One), and triggered an incendiary catalyst on the main server
with intent to destroy all program data.

Fire spread faster than Solis anticipated. West corridor collapsed
at 03:10.

CASUALTIES:
- Elena Solis: Deceased (west corridor).

SUBJECT STATUS:
- Subject Zero (Vane, E., age 7): RECOVERED ALIVE at mouth of
  ventilation shaft 3. Minor burns. Refused to leave position
  until restrained.
- Subject One (Solis, M., age 9): MISSING. Escaped through
  ventilation shaft 3 ahead of Zero. Search of eastern creek bank
  negative. Presumed dead from hypothermia or drowning.
- Subjects Two through Five: Transferred to Division 9 in 08/2004.
  Unaffected.

ASSESSMENT:
Loss of Subject One is a severe setback. The Zero–One pair is the
only pair to achieve stable two-way synchronization. Without One,
the probability of bringing Zero past the Layer 4 threshold is
significantly reduced.

DIRECTIVES:
- Continue program with Zero.
- Maintain low-level, non-public search for One.
- Seal all fire department records. Official report: gas leak
  accident.

Approved: I. Halloway, Program Director
==================================================================
```

Nobody said anything.

Elias read *refused to leave position until restrained* over and over. He didn't remember that night, but his left hand, hanging at his side, suddenly stung at the wrist, right where there was a faint burn scar he had only noticed that morning while washing his face, with no idea where it came from.

Lena scrolled down and found a second document, shorter, dated much later.

```
==================================================================
INTERNAL MEMORANDUM — PROJECT MNEMOSYNE
Date: June 3, 2010
Re: Subject Zero, next phase
==================================================================
Six years of trials since the 2004 incident have failed to bring
Zero past Layer 4. Each time the threshold is approached, the
subject's consciousness collapses. Diagnosis: insufficient
autobiographical foundation. Zero has no "life" for Layer 4 to
reconstruct. A brain that knows only a laboratory is not stable
enough.

New hypothesis: Layer 4 stabilizes only when the subject (1)
carries a mature autobiographical self, formed in real life, and
(2) enters voluntarily.

PLAN:
1. Erase Zero's memories of the Prometheus period.
2. Implant replacement foundational memories: orphan; caused the
   fire that killed his foster mother (file "Ashford/2003").
   Purpose: chronic guilt, limited social attachment, motivation
   to seek memory-removal therapy in the future.
3. Release into the foster care system. Passive monitoring.
4. When subject reaches adulthood: guide subject to VOLUNTEER for
   the Layer 4 trial.

Approved: I. Halloway
==================================================================
```

Elias read the second item in the plan, then read it again. *Caused the fire that killed his foster mother. Purpose: chronic guilt.*

He didn't remember writing that notebook, didn't remember the sleepless nights. But the afternoon before last, in that bare apartment, he had read every line of it. *The curtains caught. I stood in the corner and couldn't make a sound. She ran in. She didn't run out.* He had read *if anyone could take this out of my head, I'd give them anything.*

For twenty years, the man who wrote that notebook had punished himself for a candle that never existed. And that same guilt had led him, docile as anything, all the way to a chair in a lab. They hadn't needed to force him to do anything. They had only needed to wait.

He stepped back, and his shoulders hit the bookshelf. A few books fell onto the carpet and he didn't bother to pick them up.

"There was no candle," he said, in a voice that sounded like someone else's. "There's no Ashford house. Nobody died because of me."

He waited for relief to hit him, but it didn't come. What came instead was something cold and hollow. He'd been robbed of twenty years so he could mourn a woman who never existed, while the real woman, the one who died to save him, he couldn't even remember.

He turned to Lena, meaning to say something, but she wasn't looking at him. She was looking at the screen.

She had scrolled to the very end of the first document, to the appendix: a subject information table in two columns, each with a front-facing ID photo against a white background.

The left column was a seven-year-old boy, black hair sticking up, gray eyes, so thin his cheeks were hollow. Underneath: *Subject Zero. Vane, Elias. Neuroplasticity index: 99.8.*

The right column was a nine-year-old girl, ragged short black hair, dark eyes staring into the lens, not smiling.

> *Subject One. Solis, Maren.*
> *Born: April 22, 1995.*
> *Mother: Elena Solis (Biochemistry Lead, Prometheus).*
> *Father: Unknown.*
> *Neuroplasticity index: 99.6.*
> *Note: Admitted to the program under agreement with Lead Solis (see Appendix C). Two-way synchronization with Zero since March 2003.*

Elias looked at the photo. It was her, the girl in his head, the girl who had screamed *don't forget me*. Then he looked at the woman sitting in front of the screen.

Lena sat with her back straight, her hands on the keyboard, motionless. Her face was white in the light of the monitor, and her lips were moving without sound, as if she were reading one line again and again to be sure she hadn't misread it.

"My birthday," she said. Her voice was strangely calm, like an examiner reading out a finding. "April twenty-second, 1995. That's my birthday."

"Lena—"

"Margaret called me Maren." She still didn't look at him. "Yesterday, at the nursing home. I played her the lullaby and she sang along. Then she took my hand and called me Maren and said Thomas told her to keep me safe." She let out a short, broken laugh. "And I kept telling myself she was confused. She's been confused for three years. She calls the nurses by the name of a cat that died in '98."

She raised her hand and touched the screen, her fingertip resting on the girl's face.

"I have no memories from before I was nine," she said. "They told me I had a head injury in a car accident. My parents died, I was the only survivor, and Margaret took me in because she was close friends with the Dalys. For twenty years I believed that." Her finger trembled against the glass. "I've never seen a picture of my parents. She said they all burned up in the accident, and I never asked anything else. Why did I never ask anything else?"

Elias had no answer. All he could do was stand there and look at her. For two days she had kept a whole table between them, and now she was sitting less than an arm's length away, and he didn't dare reach out.

*Elena Solis.* The woman who sang the lullaby behind the door, the woman who pried off the vent grate with a burned arm, who died in the west corridor. She was her mother.

"My mother's name was Elena," Lena said very quietly, as though trying the words on her tongue for the first time. "My mother sang that song. My mother died so that..."

She didn't finish, and she didn't need to. *So that both of us would live.*

Elias opened his mouth. He didn't know what he meant to say. That he was sorry, or that he remembered her now, or that he remembered nothing and still knew her. None of it was right.

And before he could say anything, the lights went out.

One second of dark silence, with only the glow of the monitor. Then the building's fire alarm went off, ripping through the soundproofed room, shrill and relentless. Red emergency lights in the ceiling started to spin, sweeping red streaks across the walls, across the floor, across Lena's white face.

On the secondary monitor in the corner of the desk, the ground-floor security feed flicked on: three black vans with no plates parked at an angle in the alley behind the building, rear doors flung open, figures in black pouring out. Ballistic helmets, night-vision goggles, submachine guns with suppressors. Twelve of them. Elias counted before the screen went dead.

He turned to Lena. She was already on her feet. Her face was still white, but her eyes had changed completely. The examiner was back.

"The drive," he said.

She had ripped it out of the port before he finished the word.
