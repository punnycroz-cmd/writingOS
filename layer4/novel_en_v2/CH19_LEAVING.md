# CHAPTER 19: LEAVING

Viper didn't panic. That was the first thing Elias noticed.

He set the rag down on the table, folded in half the way he always folded it, then knelt in front of Rosie and took the jar with the dung beetle in it and examined it very seriously.

"Gus is a good-looking beetle," he said. "Can you take Gus next door and show Grandma? Grandma's never met Gus."

Rosie narrowed her eyes. "You're getting rid of me."

"I'm asking for your help. Grandma's making flan. She needs a taste tester."

"You're lying. Grandma made flan on Saturday."

Viper was quiet for a second. Then he put his two big hands on his daughter's shoulders.

"Rosie," he said, in a completely different voice. "I need you to go to Grandma's right now. Out the back door, through the hole in the fence. Tell Grandma to call Uncle Diego and say *fishing trip*. She'll understand."

Rosie looked at her father. Her lip trembled a little. Then she nodded, hugged the jar to her chest, turned, and ran out the back, her yellow boots thumping down the wooden steps and fading away.

Viper stood up and watched his daughter until she had squeezed through the hole in the fence. Only then did he turn around.

"Diego's my wife's brother," he told the room. "Firefighter up in Vermont. Has a cabin in the mountains, no cell service. Our two families agreed on it a long time ago, in case anything happened." He laughed, without any joy in it. "I figured in case I went bankrupt or something. Never figured on this."

---

They had twelve minutes to pack. Kev timed it.

Lena packed the meds and equipment into the case. Linh pulled the hard drives out of the three machines in the study and wound the cables around her arm like yarn. Kev wiped the local network and ran an overwrite on Viper's router three times in a row. Viper filled the taco truck's spare tank from the reserve can in the shed, then loaded two cases of water, a bag of bread, and the half pot of chili.

"You're seriously bringing the chili?" Linh asked.

"I only made it yesterday," Viper said, as if that explained everything.

Elias stood in the middle of the living room with no idea what to do. He had nothing to pack. A coat, a phone with no SIM card, Corwin's notebook, which Lena had given him to hold onto last week. He stood there watching everyone run back and forth, and thought: they're running because of me.

Outside, the three black dots were still circling over the end of the street, lower than before.

"Elias." Lena stood at the foot of the stairs with the case on her shoulder. "Are you okay?"

"Fine."

"You're a terrible liar."

"I know."

She looked at him one second longer, as though about to ask something, but Kev called out from the study, "Seven minutes!" and she turned away.

---

They left Ashgrove by the dirt road behind the woods, the one only hunters and kids skipping school knew about. Viper drove. The taco truck bucked over potholes, and the pots on the rack clanged like church bells. The taco sign had been taken off the roof and lay on the floor in the back between the onion boxes, still plugged in and still slowly turning, because nobody had remembered to pull the cord.

Kev sat in the passenger seat with his laptop open on his knees, eyes on the map. "The three drones are still over the town. They haven't followed us."

"Haven't yet?" Viper said.

Kev didn't answer.

In the back, Linh sat on a box of onions hugging the bag of hard drives like a cat. Lena sat on the floor with her back against the refrigerator and the case between her knees. Elias sat across from her, in exactly the spot he had sat two weeks ago, the day this truck picked them up at the laundromat. It seemed to have become his spot.

The truck turned off the dirt road onto a narrow paved road running between two rows of pines. The road was wet, and it was starting to drizzle.

"So where are we going?" Linh asked.

"I'm thinking," Kev said.

"Think faster, please. Rosie's dad is driving with no idea where to."

"I know where I'm going," Viper said. "Straight."

"Straight to where?"

"Till Kev's done thinking."

Linh laughed, a laugh as tight as a guitar string, and Lena laughed softly too. Elias didn't. He was looking at the clock on his phone.

4:50. The last transmission had been at 3:51. Every forty minutes. So 4:31 had already gone out, and the next would be 5:11, twenty-one minutes from now. How far could they get in twenty-one minutes? Wherever they got, at 5:11 his brain would put out a pulse, and the three black dots in the sky would know exactly where this lime-yellow taco truck was. Then 5:51. Then 6:31. On and on.

He looked at Lena. She was leaning her head back against the refrigerator with her eyes closed, her fingers tapping on the lid of the case. Three quick, two slow.

Tonight. He had promised himself. Tonight he would tell her. Now there wasn't going to be any tonight.

---

It was Kev who spoke first. He didn't turn around. He just said it through the hatch between the cab and the back, his voice as even as a weather report.

"Elias. It's time."

Lena opened her eyes.

Elias looked up at the little hatch and saw half of Kev's face, the black-framed glasses aimed straight ahead through the windshield.

"Kev," he said.

"You've got twenty minutes. I'm letting you go first. If you don't tell her, I will."

"Tell her what?" Linh said, looking from Kev to Elias. "Tell her *what*?"

Viper didn't say anything, but his hands tightened on the wheel.

Lena had sat up straight. She was looking at Elias, and he recognized the look in her eyes. It was the look she used on brain scans, the one that hunted for the lag, the mismatch.

"Elias?" she said.

He opened his mouth. He had been rehearsing this for ten days, in the shower, on the roof, chopping onions, washing dishes. He'd had openings ready, explanations, apologies. Now all of it was gone.

"They never needed to follow us," he said. "Not once. The chart said *neural data transmitted directly*. I thought it meant data from the machines at the hospital. It meant my brain. It sends out a signal on its own, every forty minutes. Kev picked it up ten days ago."

The truck dropped into a pothole, and the pots clanged.

"Ten days," Lena said.

"That's how they found your office. Not the black sedan. Not some informant. Me. That's how they knew about Viper's house. That's why there are drones over Rosie's house." He was talking faster now, the words pouring out like water over a dam. "Kev told me I had to tell you right away. I said wait. Wait until there's a way to mask it. I said later. And then later again."

Nobody said anything.

Linh turned to Kev. "You knew. For ten days."

"I knew," Kev said.

"You didn't tell me."

"He asked me not to."

Linh turned away and stared out the back window, where the pines were falling away into the rain. She held the bag of hard drives tighter, her mouth pressed into a straight line.

Lena didn't say anything at all. She just sat there looking at him, and there was nothing on her face. Not anger, not hurt. It was completely blank. It was the most frightening thing he had ever seen on her face.

"When's the next signal?" she asked, her voice flat.

"5:11," Kev said.

She checked the watch on her wrist. 5:02. "Nine minutes. That's not enough to do anything." She turned toward the hatch. "Marcus, keep driving. Don't stop. Kev, wherever we go, we'll have to run from it every forty minutes. Is there any way to keep them off us?"

"Keep moving. Switch vehicles. Split up." Kev paused. "Splitting up is best. One vehicle with Elias goes one way, one without him goes the other."

"Then we split."

She said it without looking at Elias. Then she pulled the case onto her lap, opened it, and began checking every vial, one by one, carefully and slowly, as if there were nothing in the world more important right now.

Elias watched her hands. Her fingers weren't tapping anymore.

---

At 5:11, Kev looked at his screen and said, "There it is."

Nobody asked anything. Nobody needed to.

At 5:24, the taco truck stopped in the lot of an abandoned gas station off the highway. A gray pickup was already parked there, the same one Kev and Linh had driven to Ashgrove ten days ago. Kev had asked someone he knew to bring it out here around noon. He always had a backup plan. Elias wondered how long Kev had been preparing for this. Maybe since the first day.

They stood under the rusted canopy over the pumps, rain dripping off its edge.

"Lin goes with me," Kev said. "Lena goes with—"

"I'll go with Linh," Lena said.

Kev looked at her, then at Elias, and nodded.

"Viper goes with Elias," Kev said. "You two head south. I'll send coordinates for each leg. Change location every forty minutes. I'll find a way to mask the signal."

"How?" Viper asked.

Kev didn't answer right away. He looked at Lena. "The only way I know needs someone who understands his brain."

Lena was hitching the case onto her shoulder. Her hand stopped on the strap for a moment, then pulled it up and settled it into place.

"Send me the technical file," she said. "I'll read it."

That was all she said. Then she walked to the gray pickup, opened the back door, got in, and shut it.

Linh followed. At the door of the truck she stopped and turned back to look at Elias, her eyes red.

"You're an idiot," Linh said, her voice shaking. "You know that? For three years I thought you never talked because you were a bot. Turns out you never talked because you're an idiot."

"Lin—"

"Next time, *say something*, okay? Say it to me. I'm not mad that they're tracking you. I'm mad you didn't tell me." She wiped her nose on her sleeve. "Idiot."

She climbed in and slammed the door.

Kev walked to the driver's side. Before he got in, he turned and looked at Elias through the rain.

"She'll read the file," Kev said. "That's the kind of person she is."

"I know."

"The rest of it, I don't know." Kev got in.

The gray pickup started, backed up, and pulled out onto the highway heading north. Elias watched it until its taillights disappeared around a bend.

Viper stood next to him with his hands in his jacket pockets and didn't say anything for a long while.

"You know," Viper said at last, "my wife used to say the worst thing a man can do isn't lie. It's keep his mouth shut and tell himself that means he didn't lie."

Elias didn't answer.

"Get in," Viper said. "Thirty-two minutes till your head goes off again. I don't want to get shot dead at this ugly-ass gas station."

They got in. The taco truck coughed to life, rattling, and turned onto the highway heading south. On the floor in the back, the taco sign was still plugged in, still slowly turning, throwing lime-yellow light across the onion boxes and across the spot where Lena had been sitting, where the unicorn blanket still held a small dent.
