# THE NAME THAT SURVIVED
## CHAPTER 10: DEEP-DIVE SCAN (FULL NOVEL EDITION)

Dawn broke through the filthy plastic blinds in dirty, sickly streaks of yellow.

Elias sat at the small laminate desk, his skin still pale, but the wild, feverish tremor in his hands had subsided into a steady, controlled calm thanks to the stabilizing serum. The titanium sensor band was secured firmly around his brow, its cryogenic nodes humming with a low, sub-audible vibration that kept his temporal temples cool to the touch.

Maren stood beside him, her fingers resting lightly on the master abort switch of the portable synthesizer. 

"We need to map their network topology," Elias said, his eyes fixed on the blank black screen of the laptop. "Halloway’s urban headquarters on Fifth Avenue is an empty shell. It’s an administrative office with dummy servers designed to absorb regulatory audits. The actual operational command nodes for *Project Mnemosyne*—the servers that control the Layer-3 military simulations and the Layer-4 quantum lattice—are hosted on isolated, off-grid hardware."

"And you think you can find the routing address through Layer 2?" Maren asked, her voice tight with professional skepticism and personal worry.

"I don't think. I know," Elias said. "When my brain was ejected from Layer 4 eleven days ago, it didn't just dump my autobiographical memory—it acted like an unshielded sponge. My subconscious downloaded an encrypted fragment of their master handshake protocol. If I dive into Layer 2, I can use that handshake like a biological magnet to trace their active data pipelines."

Maren looked at his gray eyes, then down at the biometric display on her wrist. His resting heart rate was seventy-eight. His intracranial pressure had stabilized within the low-normal range. 

"Two minutes, Elias," she said, her voice dropping into a tone that brooked no negotiation. "Not a single second more. If I see your theta-gamma coherence drift past fifty hertz, or if your systolic blood pressure climbs above one-fifty, I am pulling the physical plug. You do not argue. You do not linger."

Elias offered a faint, fleeting ghost of a smile. "Two minutes. Yes, Doctor."

"Close your eyes. Engaging Layer-2 neural synchronization in three... two... one."

Maren flicked the primary toggle.

The quantum synthesizer let out a resonant, harmonic hum that filled the cramped motel room.

*The descent was instantaneous.*

Elias did not feel as though he were falling asleep; he felt as though his consciousness were being stripped of physical mass and hurled through a fiber-optic conduit at the speed of light.

The motel room, the rain, the smell of cheap pine cleaner—all of it dissolved into a vast, boundless expanse of deep cobalt-blue space.

In Layer 2, the digital infrastructure of the metropolitan area did not appear as lines of code; it manifested as an intricate, crystalline metropolis of towering geometric lattices. Massive pipelines of glowing sapphire light pulsed beneath his virtual feet, carrying millions of commercial data packets, financial transactions, and municipal communications across the city grid.

Elias hovered in the digital ether, his avatar rendered in crisp, high-definition lines—a dark, agile figure moving with effortless grace. 

He closed his virtual eyes, reached into the center of his chest, and released the encrypted cryptographic token he had carried out of his coma.

A shock wave of golden resonance rippled outward from his hands.

Instantly, the ordinary blue lattices of the commercial internet faded into transparency, revealing a hidden, subterranean network of thick, heavily armored black conduits snaking beneath the city. 

*The Prometheus Pipeline.*

Elias latched onto the signal, following the conduit as it surged eastward, bypassing the municipal routing hubs, cutting through the industrial harbor, and heading straight out toward the dark, turbulent waters of the Atlantic coastline.

*Target acquired: Coastal Relay Station 09 — The Point Lookout Lighthouse.*

He prepared to initiate the disconnect sequence.

*And then, the universe ruptured.*

A sudden, violent gravitational distortion dragged his avatar sideways. The crystalline blue architecture of Layer 2 warped, shattering into jagged shards of dark, suffocating glass.

The digital ether vanished, replaced instantaneously by an overwhelming sensory nightmare:

*Freezing, pitch-black seawater crashing over his head.*  
*The deafening groan of fiberglass hull tearing against sharp coral teeth.*  
*The taste of brine, blood, and diesel fuel filling his mouth.*  
*A man’s face—rugged, bearded, his dark eyes wide with agony as water swallowed his lungs—reaching out through the churning foam, his voice tearing across the quantum frequency:*

***“Maren... tell her the drive is safe... she can’t let Halloway find the reef... MAREN!”***

The raw, unprocessed agony of a drowning man slammed directly into Elias's cerebral cortex. The emotional shock wave hit his hippocampus like a freight train, igniting every pain receptor in his nervous system.

"ELIAS! PULL OUT!" Maren’s voice pierced the roaring oceanic abyss, distorted and frantic.

In the physical world, Maren slammed her palm down on the emergency abort switch.

The quantum synthesizer shrieked as the power was cut.

Elias jerked violently in the chair, his back arching, his mouth opening in a silent, strangled scream as both of his nostrils erupted in thick, dark streams of blood. 

He pitched forward off the chair. 

Maren dropped to her knees, catching his heavy, trembling shoulders against her chest before his head could strike the concrete floor, holding him tightly as the room spun in terrifying, bloody chaos.
