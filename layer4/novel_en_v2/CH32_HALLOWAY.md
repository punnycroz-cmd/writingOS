# CHAPTER 32: HALLOWAY

Elias didn't remember how he ran the last two hundred yards.

He remembered Linh crying. He remembered that the spot where Viper had been lying was now just a cloud of pixel ash drifting upward, and a scrap of half-burned pink cloth. He remembered bending down to pick it up and tucking it into a gap in his chest plate, without knowing why.

Then he was standing in front of the gate, and Linh was kneeling at its foot, pressing the diamond-shaped decoder to the face of the hexagonal door, her small fingers flying across its crystal surface so fast they blurred.

"Ninety seconds," she said, her voice still thick with tears. "Sixteen layers. I need ninety seconds."

Elias turned his back to her and raised both blue blades.

On top of the gate, the three-barreled turret was recharging, the energy rings at the ends of the barrels spinning slowly, then faster. All around them, the hundreds of black animals stood still, eyes dull silver, only watching. Soldiers in black were pouring into the courtyard through the hole Viper had smashed in the wall. Five of them, ten.

"Kev," Elias said into the comms.

"On my way." A small crack from above, and the lead soldier burst into red dust. "I'm out of the building. Thirty seconds."

"The turret."

"I see it. Can't hit it. It's shielded."

The turret was fully charged, its glowing barrels pointing down. They were aimed at Linh, kneeling right at the foot of the gate with her back turned.

Elias jumped. He didn't know he could jump that high. Vanguard armor let you jump three times higher than a normal person, but the turret was five times higher. He drove one blade into the face of the gate for a foothold, kicked off it, and jumped again, swinging the second blade up just as he came level with the turret.

The energy blade hit the shield and threw sparks. It didn't get through. But it made the turret jerk, and the three red beams lanced out six feet wide of Linh and slammed into the stone courtyard, gouging three black craters.

Elias dropped, rolled, and sprang back up.

"Sixty seconds," Linh said without turning around, her hands still flying over the crystal.

---

Kev arrived with forty seconds left.

He didn't come in through the hole like Viper. He dropped from above, from the top of the third wall, his gray cloak flaring out like wings. His sniper rifle had folded down into a short double-barreled gun. He landed next to Elias on one knee and fired twice at the soldiers.

"Where's Viper?" Kev asked without looking over.

"Disconnected."

Kev fired again. "Lena will take care of him."

The two of them stood side by side with their backs to Linh, between her and the soldiers in black. Ten of them. Fifteen. Every one that fell came back three seconds later. It was like fighting a wave.

"Thirty seconds," Linh said.

A soldier slipped in between Kev and Elias. Elias cut him down, and he vanished, and three seconds later he reappeared right behind Elias with his rifle raised. Kev shot him from a foot and a half away, then spun and shot another.

"Ten seconds," Linh said.

The turret on top of the gate began charging again.

"Five."

The energy rings spun faster and faster.

"Four. Three."

Elias looked up at the turret. This time it wasn't aimed at Linh. It was aimed at him.

"Two."

"Elias," Kev said, his voice as even as ever. "Get behind me."

"One."

The gate opened without a sound. The face of the hexagonal door simply dissolved, like steam on glass, revealing darkness behind it. Linh pitched forward with nothing left to lean on.

At that exact moment, the turret fired.

Kev was already standing in front of Elias. He didn't jump or lunge. He just took one step sideways, clean and precise, like a soldier who had practiced that exact move a thousand times inside a box of light. The three red beams hit him. He didn't fall right away. He stood there one more second and looked back at Elias over his shoulder.

"Go," Kev said.

Then he broke apart into gray pixel ash, like burning paper.

> `[GHOST_KEV: FORCED DISCONNECT]`

Elias stood frozen.

"KEV!" Linh screamed. She had turned around, standing in the open doorway, staring at the gray ash rising. "Kev, no—"

Another red beam hit her in the back. It didn't come from the turret. It came from inside the gate, from the darkness.

Linh didn't scream. She just looked down at her chest, where a red hole was spreading, with the surprised face of a little kid who has just dropped her ice cream cone.

"Oh," she said. Then she looked up at Elias. "Eli. The door's open."

Then she vanished too.

> `[CIPHER_LIN: FORCED DISCONNECT]`

---

Elias stood alone on the threshold.

Behind him, the soldiers in black had stopped attacking. They stood in a still arc, rifles lowered. The black animals hadn't moved. The turret on top of the gate had gone dark. Ahead, in the darkness inside the fortress, there was light.

He walked in.

It was a huge hexagonal room with a ceiling so high he couldn't see the top. The floor was black glass, and under the glass light was flowing like rivers, millions of them, each one a fine silver thread running in from every direction and converging on the center of the room. In the very center, floating at eye level, was a glass sphere the size of a soccer ball. Something moved inside it like smoke, like lines of writing made of light. The archive. Mnemosyne. Twenty years.

And standing beside the sphere, one hand resting lightly on its surface as if on the head of a pet, was Iris Halloway.

She looked exactly as she did in real life: a tailored navy wool coat, silver hair combed straight back, a double-helix pin on her lapel. No armor, no weapon. In Layer 3, where you could be anything you wanted, she had chosen to be herself. At her feet, sitting up like a loyal dog, was a small fox that glowed pale blue. The same fox from the Somnia ad Elias had seen on the lobby screen at the hospital the morning he woke up.

"Mr. Vane," Halloway said. "Or should I call you Zero? You never did like that name."

Elias didn't answer. Both blades were still in his hands.

"Your friends are fine," Halloway said, as if giving a weather report. "Forced disconnects, that's all. A few days of headaches at most. Nobody's going to die. I'm not a monster."

"You killed Elena Solis."

"Elena killed herself, with a canister of catalyst and a principle." Halloway tilted her head. "I offered to let her walk out. She chose to stand there and sing."

Elias took one step forward. Halloway didn't step back. She smiled.

"You think you came here to take this," she said, patting the sphere, "and broadcast it to the world. It's a sweet plan. Your little friend with the cat left a trace on the relay node four days ago. Your sniper friend patched it beautifully, but four days was plenty." She looked at him. "I knew you were coming. I knew what time, and which way."

"Then why let us in?"

"Because I didn't need to stop you," Halloway said. "I needed you to come here."

She raised her other hand. It did nothing. She just raised it, like a conductor. And Elias felt it.

In the corner of his head, where the elastic line connecting him to Lena had been stretched tight the whole time, another hand had just taken hold of it. A cold, dry, sure hand. A third person's. And it began to pull.

"I don't need you to win," Halloway said. Her voice was as smooth as ever, but something in it had changed, as though the roles had finally been settled and she no longer had to pretend. "I need her connected to you. For twenty years I tried to put you into Layer 4 alone, and every time, you shattered. Because a door can't stand on just one hinge." She smiled. "Now there are two."

The line in his head went taut, and on the other end of it, he could feel her. The warmth of someone in the next room had turned into panic, like someone being dragged under water. *Lena.*

"Somnia 5.0 updates at midnight tonight," Halloway said. "Three hundred million Echoes will open gates into three hundred million people's memories, and every one of those gates will lead back here." She looked at the glass sphere, at the silver rivers under the floor. "But a gate is useless if no one is standing on the other side to open it. You're the only person who has ever stood in Layer 4. With her as your anchor, you can stand there forever."

The black animals had come into the room, without any noise. They stepped across the threshold behind Elias one by one, wolves, foxes, crows, cats, and sat down in a circle around him and Halloway. Hundreds of dull silver eyes.

"What is this?" Elias asked.

"This is the future," Halloway said. "Echoes with no owners. Mnemosyne's own Echoes. Each one is a copy of millions of Echoes out there, learned from millions of people. They know what people want, what they fear, what they remember. They're missing only one thing: permission to touch." She looked at him. "And you're going to give it to them."

The line in his head stretched a little tighter. On the other end, Lena was struggling. He could feel it, like a fingernail scraping glass.

He looked at the glass sphere, at the black animals, at Halloway's raised hand. His brain did what it always did: count, calculate, measure.

The line connecting him to Lena ran through him, and Halloway was using it to pull both of them into Layer 4. If he opened Layer 4 himself, from his own side, before she finished pulling, the force from his side would snap the line. It would throw Lena out, back to the control room, back to the real world, back into her own body. And leave him here alone, on this side, in Layer 4, with no anchor, for the second time, with a brain that Lena had told him plainly, that night in the room at Viper's house, would not survive it.

*I promise. I won't open Layer 4.*

He looked at Halloway. She was smiling, waiting, and she didn't know. She had studied him for twenty years, had read every scrap of his neural data, had planted a candle and a fear in his head. But she had never been there, in the side corridor, at the mouth of ventilation shaft three. All she had was the report.

*Refused to leave position until restrained.*

Elias lowered his blades. He closed his eyes and reached for the line in his head, for the far end of it, where she was. The line couldn't carry words. But he could knock.

*Three quick. Two slow. I'm here.*

Then he turned toward the door he had seen once before, behind the cracked wall of the spherical room, the door with the nameless light behind it.

And he opened it.
