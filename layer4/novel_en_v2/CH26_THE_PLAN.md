# CHAPTER 26: THE PLAN

The relay node sat on the seabed four hundred and sixty feet down, nine miles northeast of the point. Kev had never seen it and never would. He knew it only through numbers: signal latency, bandwidth, operating temperature. It was a steel box the size of a refrigerator lying in the dark, splitting the data stream into two branches like a fork in a road. One branch to the mainland, one out to sea.

It took him four days to open it. To be precise, two days to open it and two more to make sure whoever was on the other end had no idea it had been opened. Linh said he was paranoid. He said that was why he was still alive.

---

At 2:15 in the morning on October seventh, Nyx sent him a message. Not through Lena's phone, but straight to his, through the Reykjavik tunnel he had built with his own hands and still believed nobody knew about.

> **[N]:** Nice tunnel. Almost as good as mine.

Kev looked at the message for a long time and felt a chill run down his spine, the feeling of a soldier realizing he has been sitting in someone's crosshairs for a very long time without knowing it.

He typed:

> **Kev:** What do you want.

> **[N]:** To give you what Thomas left. The password's been said. I keep my word.

A link appeared. An archive, twenty-three gigabytes, triple-encrypted.

Kev didn't open it right away. He ran it through four scanners on an air-gapped computer he'd brought for exactly this kind of moment. Four hours. Nothing. No malware, no trackers. Just data. At six in the morning, as the sky outside the concrete building turned silver-gray, he opened it.

---

Thomas Daly's archive was ten years of work by a police officer who didn't believe official records.

Copies of the 2004 fire reports from before they were sealed. Photos of the Prometheus site taken secretly from the dirt road out back at five in the morning on October fifteenth, smoke still pouring out. Notes from interviews with three former technicians at the institute, two of whom died less than a year later. A list of names of the "secondary subjects," numbers two through five, four children, transferred to "Division 9" in August 2004.

Kev read the list twice and memorized all four names. He didn't tell anyone about them. Not yet.

Then he found the last folder, created May 2, 2012, twelve days before Thomas died.

*ECHO / HALLOWAY / SURVEILLANCE.*

Inside were recordings. Thomas had recorded his own phone. He'd set an old tape recorder next to the phone with its Echo in his bedroom, three nights running, while he pretended to sleep, and then analyzed how much data the phone sent out. His handwritten notes, scanned, sat beside each recording:

*Night 1: Echo sent 340 MB of data at 3 a.m. I didn't touch the phone all night.*

*Night 2: Talked to Ruth about N. in the bedroom (on purpose, as a test). 4 minutes later, Echo sent a small packet to an IP address belonging to Halloway Neuro-Systems. Not the general Echo server. A private server.*

*Night 3: Turned off Echo. It turned itself back on at 2:17.*

*Conclusion: Echo doesn't just listen. It chooses what to listen for. It knows which keywords to report.*

Kev sat motionless in front of the screen. Ten feet away, his gray paper crane perched on top of the server cabinet, wings folded, eyes dark, in sleep mode. He had left it that way since the day they came to Ashgrove.

He looked at it for a long time. Then he got up, walked over, picked up the crane, and turned it off. Really off, this time. He pulled the connection core out of the slot in his little computer too, something he hadn't pulled out once in seven years, since he left the program.

The paper crane lay still in his hand, a cleverly folded piece of gray paper, weighing nothing.

---

"They don't just want Layer 4," Kev said.

The five of them sat around the wooden table in the control room. Outside it was raining, heavy sea rain lashing the concrete walls. Viper had made fish soup, and nobody was eating.

Kev turned his laptop toward them. On the screen was an internal Halloway Neuro-Systems document that he and Linh had pulled last night from the data branch running to the mainland.

*SOMNIA 5.0 — LAUNCH 10/14/2024*
*Headline feature: DEEP ECHO*

"Launching October fourteenth," Linh said. "She picked that date on purpose."

"What's Deep Echo?" Viper asked.

Kev scrolled down. "Right now everybody's Echo runs in Layer 1. It listens, it learns, it helps. Version 5.0 lets it go deeper, down into Layer 2, all the way into memory." He paused. "The marketing says it's to help users 'reorganize painful memories' and 'relive beautiful moments.' There's a whole promo video. An old lady asks her Echo to help her find the memory of her wedding day."

"Doesn't sound so bad," Viper said.

"Read the technical section."

Lena already had. She'd finished first, because she understood the terminology, and her face was white.

"Every Echo becomes a gate," she said. "A gate into the user's Layer 2. Three hundred million Somnia users, three hundred million gates." She looked at Kev. "And all of those gates connect back to one place."

"Mnemosyne," Kev said.

"Layer 4 can overwrite every layer beneath it," Elias said, his voice low. He understood that better than anyone. "If Mnemosyne can run Layer 4 stably, and then connect to three hundred million gates..."

Nobody finished the sentence. Nobody needed to.

"But she doesn't have a stable Layer 4," Linh said. "Right? Eli's the only person who's ever been in, and it nearly killed him."

Kev scrolled one more page. A diagram: two circles joined by a straight line, with a double-helix symbol in the center.

*PAIRED CONFIGURATION — PHASE 2*
*Subject Zero (operator) + Subject One (anchor)*
*Projected: Layer 4 stable at 94.7% with a two-way synchronized anchor.*

Below it was a note dated September fifth, two days after Halloway came to Lena's office.

*Subject One identified. Currently with Subject Zero. Natural synchronization in progress (see Alpha-7 data). DO NOT INTERVENE. Allow process to complete on its own.*

Lena read the line, and Kev saw her hand tighten on the edge of the table.

"They let us go," she said. "At St. Jude. The man on the fire escape lowered his gun. I kept wondering why." She laughed, without any joy. "They didn't want to catch us. They wanted us together. Every dive we did together, every time we remembered something together, we were doing their work for them."

"The signal's off," Elias said. "Since the warehouse. They don't know where we are anymore."

"They don't need to," Kev said. "Not now. On the night of the fourteenth, when Somnia 5.0 goes live, that's when they need the two of you. That's when they'll come looking."

---

The plan was drawn on the back of an old nautical chart that Viper turned over. Kev drew it in ballpoint: straight lines, arrows, timestamps crammed into the margins.

Mnemosyne, the real server, was nowhere near Halloway headquarters on Fifth Avenue. It lived inside a Layer 3 fortress, a simulated space running on military hardware somewhere on the mainland. The cable branch running from the seabed relay node to the mainland was one of only three ways in.

"The night of the fourteenth, 11:45," Kev said. "Fifteen minutes before Somnia 5.0 auto-updates for three hundred million people. Their system will be putting everything into the launch. Their defenses will be at their thinnest."

"We go in," Linh said, pointing at the diagram. "Eli, Viper, Kev, and me. Four of us. Layer 3. Dive from here, follow the undersea cable, straight into the fortress."

"The target is the original archive," Kev said. "All of it. Prometheus, Mnemosyne, Thomas's recordings, the Echo logs, Somnia 5.0. Once we've got it, we push it out: the press, the FBI, the Justice Department, the congressional committee investigating Halloway's military contracts. Nyx has channels to all of them."

"And it has to happen before Somnia 5.0 goes live," Lena said. "If it's running by midnight, anything we put out will be too late."

"What about you?" Linh asked.

Everyone looked at Lena.

"I stay here," she said. "In the real world. At the console."

"You're not diving?"

"I'm the anchor." Lena looked at Elias. "Kev ran the numbers. Without an anchor, Elias's brain can take about twelve minutes of Layer 3 before he starts bleeding. With me synced from outside, thirty minutes."

"That's exactly what Halloway wants," Elias said. He was on his feet. "You next to me, synced with me, connected to their network. Exactly what's in that diagram."

"I know."

"Then don't do it."

"Without me you have twelve minutes. Getting into the fortress takes eight. Twelve isn't enough." Lena's voice was as even as if she were reading out a forensic finding. "With me, you have thirty. That's enough."

"I don't need enough. I need you not to get pulled in."

"Elias." Lena stood up and faced him across the table. "They need both of us, right? Then that's the one thing they don't have. If I'm synced with you but still awake, out here in the real world, sitting at a console I control, they only get half. And half is useless."

"What if they pull you in anyway?"

"Then you pull me out." She paused. "The way I pulled you out at the warehouse."

Nobody said anything. The rain kept lashing the concrete.

Kev watched the two of them across the table. He'd seen this before, inside the box of light, two years in one week. He'd seen soldiers argue over who got to go first through a door that might be mined. Everyone wanted to go first. Not because they were brave, but because they couldn't stand to watch the other one go first.

"One condition," Kev said.

They both turned to him.

"Elias, you don't open Layer 4." Kev looked straight at him. "No matter what happens. Even if Halloway is standing right in front of you. Even if all four of us go down. You don't open it. Lena's connected to you. You open it, and you hand them exactly what they need."

Elias looked at Kev, then at Lena.

"I promise," he said.

"Promise her," Kev said. "Not me."

Elias turned to Lena. She was looking at him, both hands braced on the table, the ring on its chain swinging in front of her chest.

"I promise," he said, more quietly. "I won't open Layer 4."

Lena nodded once and sat down.

Viper cleared his throat. "Soup's gone cold," he said. "Anybody wants it, I'll heat it up."

Nobody answered. Viper got up and heated the soup anyway. That was how he dealt with everything, and Kev was starting to understand it wasn't a bad way at all.

---

That night, Kev was sitting alone in the control room checking the connections for the eleventh time when his phone buzzed.

> **[N]:** Good plan. Three holes.

Kev looked at the message without surprise. He'd stopped being surprised by this person a while ago.

> **Kev:** Go on.

> **[N]:** 1. They won't just defend inside Layer 3. They'll come to the lighthouse. Real people.
> **[N]:** 2. Your friend, the girl with the bubble cat. She left a trace on the relay node. Small. But enough.
> **[N]:** 3. Elias Vane won't keep his promise.

Kev read the last line three times.

> **Kev:** How do you know.

> **[N]:** Because I watched him sit in front of a ventilation shaft when he was seven. I have the Prometheus security footage from that night. Thomas kept it.
> **[N]:** People like that don't change.

Kev set the phone down and looked at the closed steel door, listening to the waves outside. Then he picked the phone up again.

> **Kev:** So what can you do.

A long pause. Thirty seconds. A minute.

> **[N]:** I'll be there. My way.

The screen went dark. Kev sat there for a long time, until Linh appeared in the doorway with two cups of hot tea and sat down on the floor against the leg of his chair the way she did every night.

He didn't tell her about the second hole. He spent the next two hours patching it, while she fell asleep against his knee.
